# Agent instructions

This file is the repo law for agents and contributors. `README.md` is the public face: what nostakes is and how it is built, for someone who has never seen it. Setup and the checks live in `docs/development.md`, and deploys in `docs/deploying.md`; keep instructions out of the README. `VISION.md` says what the product is for. Read it before you design anything. It holds the commands, the validation rules, the architecture constraints, and the stop rules. The fence (types, lint, hooks, CI) enforces most of it. Fence wins over prose.

nostakes is a web app where groups of friends play poker online, at most 9 players per room. Chip accounting must be exactly right.

## Packages

- `@nostakes/engine` (`packages/engine`): pure poker rules for button-and-board games: hold'em and Omaha, no limit and pot limit. A variant is a row of data in `src/variants.ts` (`VARIANTS`: how many hole cards a seat is dealt and how many of them a hand plays), plus the betting limit in the table rules; `VARIANTS.md` names every seam and where the next variants (five-card Omaha, fixed limit, short deck, hi-lo) plug in. `tools/differential` replays seeded hands of every game and limit in PokerKit and compares them in full (`pnpm --filter @nostakes/engine differential`, run in Verify).
- `@nostakes/ledger` (`packages/ledger`): buy-ins, cash-outs, and settle-up transfers
- `@nostakes/room` (`packages/room`): one poker night as a pure reducer over the engine, the ledger, and the shuffle: members, host, seats, clocks, the queue at the end of a hand, views, and redaction. A member who leaves the room keeps a working pass and a per-person settlement. The view lists them in `departed` with their name and face until they join again, marks their seat `leavingRoom` while it plays out the hand, and still names them in `you` on their own device. The host may remove a member, or someone who left (`RemoveMember`, `MemberRemoved`, `removeMember` in `src/members.ts`), never themselves (`RemovingYourself`). A removal causes exactly the batch the person's own `LeaveRoom` would have, with `MemberRemoved` for `MemberLeft`: a seat between hands is cashed out at once, a seat in the hand plays it out on the leaving rules and stands up when it ends, and their ledger line stays in every settlement. They move to `removed`, `Join` refuses them for good (`RemovedFromRoom`), the views list the removed who have a ledger line in `removed` so the settle-up can name them, and `removedView` (`removedStandings` for a halted room) gives a removed person their own line, whether their seat is still finishing a hand, and, once the room is closed, only the transfers that involve them. `test/room.model.test.ts` checks the batch against the leave and that the line never changes afterwards. The host may also clear the room in one batch (`ClearRoom`, `clearRoom`). The command names the people the host's view listed (`players`, from `clearableOf`), and the room removes, each through `removeMember`, only those named who can still go (`clearable`): a member other than the host who holds no seat, or someone who left without a ledger line. A named person who sat down or is now in the settle-up stays, and nobody the host did not name is touched, so someone who came back between the host's view and the command stays. Then the room locks, so the same people cannot walk back in. It refuses an empty list, and a list of which nobody can still go in a room already locked (`NobodyToClear`). The model test checks over generated nights, with named lists that are current, stale, partial, or foreign, that the removed are exactly the named who could still go, that none of their lines moves, and the lock, and `test/removal.test.ts` pins a member coming back before the clear lands. While the host's `locked` setting is on, `Join` refuses anyone the room has not seen (`RoomLocked`), and someone who left still comes back with their pass. While the host's `approval.joins` setting is on (off by default), a `Join` from someone the room has not seen records `Knocked` instead (`src/door.ts`): the person is not a member, holds no name, and counts toward no cap until the host answers. `LetIn` (from the host, or whoever took over as host) causes `KnockAnswered` and exactly the batch their own join would cause at that moment, or ends the knock as `NameTaken` when a member took that name meanwhile, because a waiting knock reserves no name (a reservation would tell a stranger which names wait at the door); a full room refuses it (`TooManyPeople`) and the knock keeps waiting. `TurnAway` ends a knock, one nobody answers lapses after the host's `knockSeconds` (300 by default) through the deadline `KnockLapses` (`LapseKnocks`), and locking the room turns away everyone waiting (`Locked`). Someone who left comes back without knocking, a removed person cannot knock, a locked room takes no knocks, at most `MOST_WAITING` (3) wait at once (`TooManyKnocking`), and a night takes at most `MOST_KNOCKS` (100) knocks (`TooManyKnocks`), which bounds the log and the server's passes. Only the host's view lists who is knocking (`knocking`); every audience sees `Knocked` and `KnockAnswered` as their time alone. `knockView` gives a knocker their own name, face, the room's code, and the answer (`Waiting`, `TurnedAway`, `Lapsed`, `Locked`, `NameTaken`, or `NightOver` when the night ends first), and nothing once they are let in; `holdsPlace` is false for a removed pass and for a knock that ended without coming in. `test/knocks.test.ts` checks over generated nights with approval on that only the host sees who knocks, that letting someone in equals their join, and that a refusal touches no member, seat, or ledger line. A room holds at most `MOST_PEOPLE` (20) members and people who left (`TooManyPeople`, held by `invariants`); the removed do not count, so a leaked link fills a room only as far as the host can clear it. The room takes `RoomCodeSchema` from `@nostakes/protocol`, the one source for a room code's format. A name (`NameSchema`, `src/names.ts`) is plain single-line text chosen by Unicode category: 1 to 40 UTF-16 units of letters, marks, numbers, punctuation, symbols, and spaces, with joiners (`Join_Control`) allowed inside emoji and words, in normal form C, with no control, format, or other default-ignorable character, no space at either end, no mark first, at most four marks in a row, at least one letter or emoji, and no mix of Latin, Greek, and Cyrillic letters. `nameProblem` names the first rule a name breaks, and the page words it. The avatar string refuses the same control and invisible characters (`isPlainText`). No two people the room names, members, those who left, and the removed who have a ledger line, share a name as `sameName` compares them: the same `nameKey` (compatibility normalisation, case folded, invisible characters dropped, every run of spaces as one), or the same `nameSkeleton`, which decomposes the name and maps the 53 Latin, Greek, and Cyrillic look-alike letters in `lookAlikes` (credited in `PROVENANCE.md`) to the Latin letter they read as, a capital I to a small l, so a Cyrillic "АВС" clashes with "ABC" and "BiII" with "Bill". `Join` and `SetProfile` refuse a taken one with `NameTaken`, and `invariants` checks it pairwise. The skeleton does not catch look-alikes outside the table or in other scripts, digits for letters (0 for O), letter pairs that read as one (rn for m), or a name that reads the same only in some other font. Someone who left keeps their name, because they still appear in the settle-up and may come back with their pass; someone removed before they bought in gives theirs back, because nothing names them any more. In a room whose `shuffle` setting is `Checked` (the default for a new room), `src/sealing.ts` seals each hand (`SealDue`, `SealHand`, `HandSealed`) and deals it from the sealed beacon round (`SealedDealDue`, `DealSealed`), verifying the round's signature against the chain in `RoomOpened`, and refusing a deal without the beacon (`BeaconUnavailable`) before the sealed round's time plus the server's full wait (`beaconGiveUpTime` from `@nostakes/shuffle`: five periods, 15 s on quicknet), so the claim can never be an instant escape; the view counts the night's sealed deals by kind in `fairness.tonight`; `src/openings.ts` sends every shown card's opening to exactly its audience; `src/witness.ts` turns what one device saw into `judgeHand`'s input. `src/standings.ts` settles a room stopped by a fault: `standingsOf` cancels a hand in play as ending a stalled night does and closes the room, so the server can show a halted room's standings without storing anything. Once a seal binds, the sealed seats are frozen until the hand is dealt, and the end-of-hand queue never runs in a batch that deals hole cards (`QueueDue`, `RunQueue`). A sealed hand is dealt with the posting seats and the table rules its seal used: a posting choice made after the seal, or a rules change (queued while a hand is sealed), applies from the next hand. `test/deadlines.test.ts` checks that the room accepts the server's answer to every deadline it names, because a refused one stops the game for good, and that whenever the room would take a queue run, a deal, or a seal from the server it names a deadline for it, because a room that names none waits forever. Generated nights never reach a queue left waiting (a hand that ends in the batch that dealt it); `test/sealing.test.ts` builds that one by hand. Ending the night (`CloseRoom`, `src/closing.ts`) closes at once between hands. During a hand, or while a sealed hand is bound to be dealt, it is queued and the hand finishes on its clocks; the host can act for any seat. Once the server has marked the room stalled (`NoteStall`, `TableStalled`, cleared by `StallCleared` when the room next accepts a deadline), any member may end the night and it closes at once: a hand in play is cancelled with `HandCancelled`, which gives every dealt-in seat back the stack it held when the hand was dealt, so every chip in the pot, blinds and dead money included, goes back to the seat that put it in, chips queued for the end of the hand are dropped, and a binding seal is withdrawn. The view's `closing` (`Now`, `AfterHand`, `CancelsHand`) says which of these ending the night does at that moment. Attendance also notes the host: `HostAway` when the host has no live socket and `HostBack` when they return, and any other member may `ClaimHost` once the host has been away for `HOST_CLAIM_AFTER_SECONDS` (120), which the view shows as `hostAway.claimableAt`, so a night whose host vanished can still be ended and settled. `test/ending.test.ts` ends sampled states of generated nights, including planted sealed deals the room refuses and hands whose deadlines it refuses, within 400 commands from the host, and checks the settlement against the buy-ins and cash-outs in the log and every cancelled hand's returns against the stacks when it was dealt. Where nobody new may join (the room is locked or full), someone who left comes back with their pass to end an empty room or to take over from a vanished host, because removal and the lock make those states reachable; a vanished host whose only companions were removed is left to the host, since nobody else holds a working pass, and the first property already ends that night from the host. `test/door.test.ts` pins both comebacks as examples. A checked hand costs about 18 ms of BLS checking in `decide`, so the generic room properties make one room in twenty checked. `packages/shuffle/DESIGN.md` section 12 records the design as built. `src/signals.ts` names the signals a room relays outside its log, `RoomSignalSchema`: `Peeking` on or off, and a `Reaction` from the fixed set of nine in `REACTIONS` (Clap, Laugh, Wow, Cry, Fire, Dead, Fish, Eyes, Bird; Bird is the middle finger, drawn as the plain yellow 🖕 with no skin tone), which may name a target in `at` to throw it at that person's face. `signalAllowed` says who may send one: a peek only from a member whose seat holds hole cards in the hand in progress, and only while the `peeking` setting is on; a reaction from any member, seated or watching; and a thrown reaction only at another member who holds a seat, never at the sender, someone who left, someone removed, or a member standing. `test/signals.test.ts` checks over generated nights that a throw is allowed exactly when the sender's own view lists the target as another member in a seat. `src/moments.ts` recognises a hand worth talking about once it ends (`momentsOf`): a big hand (a nut flush or full house in a pot of 20 big blinds, quads, a straight or royal flush), a bluff shown, a hero call, a bad beat, a cooler, a suckout, the seven-deuce, the same hand chopped all in, a board run twice that split, and a scoop (`Scoop`, one player taking the main pot on both boards of a double board at a showdown with their hand tabled). On a double board each board is judged as a showdown of its own (`boardShowdowns`): a bad beat, suckout, cooler, or same hand on one board names it in `board` (1 or 2, absent on a single board), counts the board's chance with the other board's cards as dead, and a bad beat or cooler needs a loser who won no part of the main pot on either board; a hero call on a double board needs the caller to win the main pot on both boards with a hand that was weak on each, a bluff must hold nothing of its own on either board, and split runs are not recognised, since two boards are not a run twice. The hand story starts a double board with both boards, and a double board's replay says `boards: 2`. It reads only the events a spectator receives for that hand (`src/hand-story.ts`), never a hole card nobody showed, and `test/moments.test.ts` checks that a spectator and two players picked per hand compute the same moments, and on generated double boards that every player does. Each moment has a tier from 1 to 3; `ranked` orders them by tier, then puts a moment about two seats (`namesTwoSeats`) before one about one seat, so a nut flush that is also a cooler is told as the cooler with both players, then by kind. `headline` picks what the table shows under the host's `moments` setting (`All`, `SkipPainful` without bad beats and coolers, or `Off`). `src/equity.ts` gives each tabled hand's share of the boards still to come when the money went in: exact when the count fits `EQUITY_BUDGET` hand evaluations, otherwise that many from a sample seeded by the cards, so every device gets the same number; `dead` names cards known elsewhere, the other board of a double board, which leave the deck and join the seed. A bad beat, suckout, or cooler first looks at the first `QUICK_SWING_BOARDS` boards of that same sample and counts the rest only when a swing could hold within `QUICK_SWING_MARGIN` points. `src/recap.ts` folds the night's recap from the events a spectator receives (`tallied`): hands played, the time from the first deal to the last hand's end, the biggest pot and who took it, and the best moments ordered by tier, then pot, then the earlier hand, at most one per hand. It keeps the three best gentle and the three best painful moments, so `recapOf` applies the host's `moments` setting at the close without recounting, and it holds one hand's events and a fixed set of fields however long the night runs. `evolve` keeps the tally, at about 0.13 ms a hand on average and under 20 ms for the worst preflop all-in on the home-game nights of `scripts/moments.ts`. `evolveWithoutRecap`, `evolveAllWithoutRecap`, and `replayWithoutRecap` skip it where nobody reads the recap: the steps `decide` simulates, and the generated nights of every room property except the recap's own (`playSession` takes `"Folded"` to keep it), so the recap does not slow the room's other tests. The settled view carries the recap. `test/recap.test.ts` checks that it is the same for a spectator and every player, and that every card in it was shown in its own hand. `src/replay.ts` tells a hand for the moment picture, the slip, and the night reel (`replayOf`): posts, the deal, each action, the chips gathered into the pot, every street of every run, each card the table saw from the event that showed it, mucks, and the awards. A replay is television, so it may show from its first frame any hole card the table was shown by the end of the hand: `tabled` lists those cards per seat. The hard rule is that a card nobody showed is never in a replay at all: it reads cards only from events that show them, never from `HoleCardsDealt`, and `test/replay.test.ts` checks over generated hands that every card a replay holds (`cardsHeldBy`) was in the spectator's feed by the end of the hand, on every device, that no board card and no odds counted on one come before the event that dealt it, and that every seated player's device tells exactly the spectator's replay; the odds, outs, and narration are not tested. It gives the chance of each tabled hand (`Odds`) from the deal, after every fold, and after every board card, the flop one card at a time and each run on its own, but only while every seat still in the hand tabled both cards by the end, so a chance never ignores a live hand nobody saw. With three or four board cards out an `Odds` step also counts each trailing seat's outs: every unseen card that would put that seat alone ahead, counted from the tabled hands and the board only, which the test recounts. A double board gets an `Odds` step per board (`run` 1 or 2) at the deal and after every fold, and one for a board after each of its own cards; each counts the other board's cards dealt so far as `dead` (the step carries them, `OddsAsked.dead` passes them on, and the equity seed includes them), so its chance and its outs never count a card that is on the other board, and `test/replay.test.ts` checks on generated double boards that no odds step counts a card, dead ones included, before the table saw it. A `Bounty` step tells a seven-deuce bounty with what each seat paid, after the cards the rule showed, and `bombAnte` names a bomb pot's ante, whose posts are all antes and whose flop comes before any action. It takes the odds as a function (`Chances`, `tableChances` by default), and `oddsAskedBy` names every question it asks, in order, so a device can work them out elsewhere; the test checks that answers from elsewhere tell the same replay. The audiences and the redaction live in `src/redaction.ts`, so `evolve` can redact without a cycle through the views. Its `verify` plays home-game nights in `scripts/moments.ts`, prints how often each moment leads, and fails when a night of 100 hands averages outside 2 to 12 moments or the loudest tier leads more than once in two nights.
- `@nostakes/shuffle` (`packages/shuffle`): the provably fair shuffle, pure, used by the room for checked hands. It seals two decks per hand as Merkle trees of salted cards, orders the deal from a drand quicknet beacon round named in the seal, checks drand's BLS signature, judges from a device's own clock whether the seal arrived before its round, lists sealed hands with no deal, and gives one verdict per hand (`judgeHand`). The seal names how many hole cards each seat is dealt (`holeCardCount`, absent for two) and hashes it into the seed, so a server cannot read the sealed deck under another layout after the beacon. `DESIGN.md` there holds the threat model, the margins, and the cheat table.
- `@nostakes/avatar` (`packages/avatar`): the players' faces, pure, in the House style: every face is a stack of paper sheets cut from polygons with eased corners. An avatar is a Schema of parts (species, colour, suit, card, eyes, mouth, headwear, eyewear, neck, mark, extra) that encodes to a 13-character `h1` code inside the room's 512-character avatar string (`codeFor`, `AvatarFromCode`). The cast is eight species: great white, hammerhead, goldfish, puffer, piranha, angler, whale, and octopus. Each wears a card with the avatar's rank and suit (a shark's fin, a goldfish's crest, the angler's bait), and its pupils, pendant, and fold are that suit's pips. `avatarFor` reads any stored string: an `s1` code from the shark art keeps its species and wardrobe, and anything else, including an old `c1` code, an emoji, or garbage, gets `randomAvatar(seedFrom(text))`, the same face every time. `render(avatar, { expression, size, theme, layers })` returns an SVG string for six table expressions: `Resting`, `LookingDown`, `Thinking`, `Happy`, `Gutted`, and `Folded`. `theme` (`Light` by default) picks the ground colours the cuts and shadows are drawn for, and `layers` wraps each sheet in `<g class="sheet" style="--sheet:N">` so the face builder can pull the stack apart. Every lifted sheet in `src/sheets.ts` is four paths: one flat offset shadow whose depth grows with the sheet's place in the stack (fins 0.9, body 1, white card 1.35, ink 1.7, gold 1.9, all times 1.15), a hairline gap in the ground colour, a light top edge, and the fill, each sheet turned a little off true. A shadow never falls below 1.4 screen pixels, and below 56 px it darkens into an offset edge at 1.5 px, so the stack still reads at 40 px. Eight parts have a place on the face (`PLACED_PARTS`: the card's rank, eyes, mouth, headwear, eyewear, neck, mark, extra) and three do not (`PLACELESS_PARTS`: species, colour, and suit, whose pips ride on other parts' sheets). Every piece carries the part it belongs to (`ofPart` in `src/sheets.ts`, applied per layer in `src/layers.ts` and to each species' card in `src/species.ts`), and `render` takes four more options for the face builder: `parts` wraps each part's sheets in `<g data-part="...">`; `uncovered` shows one part even where another part hides it (eyes behind shades) and sets every part that covers it aside, shifted clear and faded, with the shift in `data-shift`; `lifted` does the same and also draws the part picked up, raised with a deeper shadow (`"suit"` stands for the card in both); and `window` crops the drawing to a box. `boxOfPart` gives a worn part's box, `reachOfPart` where any choice of it goes on a species, `coversOfPart` the parts that hide at least a fifth of it (sampled against the cut polygons, so glasses you can see through do not count), `spotsOfPart` points on a worn part's own larger sheets, and `closeUpOf` the window for a close-up of that part. With these options off the output is byte for byte what it was: `test/frozen.test.ts` digests 4,500 drawings of every choice on every species against the digest of the approved art. The output has no ids, `url(#)`, filters, or blend modes, so nine faces share a page cheaply; a fold is cut geometrically (`clipBelow`, `foldDown`) instead of with a clip path. The shapes, the sheet order, the palette, and the shadow rule are ported from the House design scripts; the art is original and drawn in code.
- `@nostakes/protocol` (`packages/protocol`): the JSON frames between a room's clients and its server, the room pass, and the wire limits. Beside commands and events it carries signals, such as a peek or a reaction: a client sends `Signal`, and the server relays it to the room's other live sockets as `Signal { from, signal }`. A signal is never stored, sequenced, replayed on reconnect, or answered, and losing one breaks nothing. `SIGNALS_PER_SECOND` and `SIGNAL_BURST` bound each player's signals. A room meters frames by its own clock as it processes them, so after the room stalls it sees the frames that queued meanwhile as one burst; each allowance's burst therefore adds its rate times `ROOM_STALL_TOLERANCE_MS` (3 s). `FRAME_ALLOWANCE` (`FRAME_BURST` plus `FRAMES_PER_SECOND` times the tolerance, 70 frames at 20 a second) meters a socket that has not said Hello. Once it has, the room meters the player across all their sockets: `PLAYER_FRAME_ALLOWANCE` is `SOCKETS_PER_PLAYER` (3) times `FRAME_ALLOWANCE`, because every device pings and signals on its own, and `COMMAND_ALLOWANCE` meters the player's commands apart, `COMMAND_BURST` (12) plus `COMMANDS_PER_SECOND` (2) times the tolerance, 18 commands at 2 a second, because commands are what the room stores and a person taps far less than that. A new socket for the same player gets no fresh budget. - `@nostakes/client` (`packages/client`): the headless room client. The clip is one shot with no cuts, 10.5 s (`BEATS` in `src/features/moments/build.ts`), that builds the head-to-head picture in story order and holds the finished picture for its last 2.5 s and more: the hook (0 to 1.6 s: the player who acted last, their panel, cards, and the board as it stood when the money went in, its last card landing at 0.6 s; a bluffer's and a lone winner's cards stay face down), the bet (1.6 to 3.2 s: their bet tab, "ALL IN 1,550", slams on and the pot counts up), the pause (3.2 to 5.2 s: the other player's panel slides in with the cards face down, "1,550 to call. Dev thinks."), the hit (5.2 s: the answer tab, CALL or FOLD, the cards turn over on the beat, the rest of the board runs out one card at a time with the flag on the deciding card, and the frame shakes once, the only camera move), the count (6.4 s: hand names, WINS, the verb, and each swing; faces change to their result), and the picture (7.6 s: the kicker, the headline stamps, the line and the footnote; from about 7.9 s every frame is exactly the picture Picture and Story share). `storyOf` (`scene.ts`) picks who acts first (the last of the two principals to bet or raise), their bet, the answer, the cards dealt before it (`hookCards`), the deal order, the pots, and the captions, which state only what the record holds. `buildPlan` is pure: it turns the picture's parts and a few extra pieces (card backs, the early faces, the bet and answer tabs, each pot amount of the count-up, the captions) into film shots with keyframed poses, each listing the cards it shows face up (`shows`) and the time each board card lands (`dealt`), and writes the `ScoreSheet` on the same beats; under reduced motion the calm cut fades everything in place and does not shake. A film (`src/features/moments/film.ts`) is everything that turns into a clip: a size, a rate of 24, 25, or 30 frames a second, a length, shots (each a piece of SVG art in film pixels, its box, and keyframed poses with an ease, hidden before its first key), an optional camera whose keys move every shot that is not pinned, and an optional mono or stereo mix. `filmFile` (`src/client/moments/film-file.ts`) is the one entry that turns any film into an MP4, and it owns the bitmaps and sound buffers it is given: it rasterizes each distinct piece of art once, a band at a time in idle slots (`clip-layers.ts`, packed by `src/features/moments/atlas.ts`), and a worker (`clip-worker.ts`) draws each frame from the keys, writes the MP4 through the `Media` bindings (Mediabunny): H.264 from the browser's `VideoEncoder` at a variable bitrate (`videoBitrate` in `film.ts`, which the worker applies: 0.013 bits a pixel a frame plus 0.0047 times the film's motion, the mean share of the frame it redraws from one frame to the next (`motionOf`), at least 0.5 Mbps and up to 3 Mbps while the picture stays within 1.4 MB, so a 10.5 s vertical clip comes to about 1.1 to 1.2 MB and keeps its sharp last frame and its sliding panels above 36 dB in Chromium; neither engine offers a quantizer mode for H.264), and the mix as AAC (160 kb/s stereo, 96 kb/s mono) from the browser's `AudioEncoder`, or, where the browser has none (Firefox), from FFmpeg's encoder in `@mediabunny/aac-encoder`. `aacEncoding` answers `Polyfill` only once the extension has encoded a probe in that worker; where even it cannot run, because a policy refuses its `blob:` worker or its WebAssembly as a browser without `'wasm-unsafe-eval'` does, it answers `None` and the clip ships silent, never failed. Mediabunny measures each encoder's priming and writes the edit list that skips it, so the mix's first sample plays at the first frame. It pads the sound to whole AAC frames, up to 23 ms past the last frame, so the writer cuts the movie and the sound track to the film's length in their headers (`playingFor` in `mediabunny/exact-length.ts`), and the file plays exactly the mix's length. A frame taken straight from a canvas comes out of both browsers' encoders as full-range video, which a player that ignores the range flag shows with the paper blown to white and the ink crushed, so the worker converts each drawn frame to limited-range BT.709 itself (`writeI420` in `src/features/moments/i420.ts`) and hands it over tagged with BT.709 primaries and matrix, the sRGB transfer the art is drawn in, and limited range (`LIMITED_BT709`). It redraws only the areas that changed since the frame before (`changedAreas` in `film.ts`: every moved shot's bounds before and after, two pixels wider and kept to even pixels for the chroma planes, joined while that saves area, and the whole frame once they cover more than 55% of it), then reads back and converts only those areas (`writeI420Area`); a frame where nothing moved is not drawn or converted at all. The worker reads a redrawn frame back through a `VideoFrame` of its canvas into one reused buffer, in the frame's own byte order because WebKit answers in BGRA whatever format is asked, rather than a new `getImageData` per frame. The worker hands back the finished file as a `Blob`, so it is never copied whole in the page. A clip's workers are never terminated while they may still be loading their modules: WebKit can crash the page when a worker is torn down with a module script load pending, so a finished or abandoned making asks its worker to close itself (`closedPolitely` in `worker-close.ts`), which the worker can only hear once its modules have run, and terminates it only if it is still open 20 s later. `filmFile` can also redraw whole frames, or draw every frame a second time whole and count the luma pixels that differ (`FilmChecks`), and `scripts/moments/film.html` makes a test film that way (`scene=moment` or `scene=replay` for a sample hand's clip, `frame=Story` for its vertical cut, `bitrate` to override the target, `refs` for the frames drawn straight from the film at those times, `fps=24` to re-time any scene, `scale` for a cut drawn smaller, `scene=pan` for a camera pan over a nine-seat table with `width`, `height`, `seconds`, and `sound=stereo`, `scene=sync` for two one-frame flashes with a click under each) and reports the time, the size, the frames redrawn, and the comparison. `clipScales` (`src/features/moments/clip-reach.ts`) reads `navigator.deviceMemory` where the browser reports it (Chromium does, Safari does not): below 2 GB no clip, from 2 GB two thirds of the full cut (720 by 1280, `LIGHT_CUT_SCALE`, the same art scaled in `shotAtlas`), and from 8 GB, or when nothing is reported, the full cut first and then two thirds, each within a budget of 45 s (`CLIP_BUDGET_MS`). - Bomb pots across the packages: a house rule in the table rules, `bombPot`, `NoBombPots` by default or `BombPots` with an `ante` in chips, `everyHands`, and `boardCount` (1 by default, or 2 for a double board). A hand is a bomb pot when its number is a multiple of `everyHands` (0 turns the schedule off) or when the host called one (`CallBombPot { on }`, `BombPotCalled`, `callBombPot` in `packages/room/src/dealing.ts`; the host can take the call back, a bomb pot spends it, and turning the rule off drops it). The engine decides it in `startHand` (`bombPotDue`, `startBombPot` in `src/forced-bets.ts`) and marks the hand `HandStarted.bombPot { ante, bigBlind }`. In a bomb pot nobody posts a blind and no seat straddles (`StraddleNotAllowed` with `BombPot`); the bomb ante replaces the table's ante, whoever pays that in other hands; every seat dealt in posts the ante as an `AntePosted`, or all it holds when it holds less, which makes side pots before any card is seen, and the part of an ante no other seat could match comes back as in any hand. There is no betting before the flop: the flop is dealt at once, before any choice of runs, so every run shares it (`owesItsFlop`), and the betting opens on the flop with the first seat left of the button that can act, the smallest bet the big blind; from there the hand is ordinary. Every seat with chips that is not sitting out is dealt in, whatever blind it owes or waits for, and what each seat owes is the same after the hand: a bomb pot cannot be used to skip a blind and cannot charge one. The button and the blinds do not move for it either: the hand keeps the rotation it found (`Hand.paused`, `rotationAfter`, which a cancelled hand uses too), and the bomb pot has a button of its own, the next seat after the last bomb pot's button or else after the button (`bombButton`), so two bomb pots in a row do not give one seat the button twice. A bomb pot pays no seven-deuce bounty, because nobody chose to play the hand. A checked hand's seal fixes its kind: the seal's seats are worked out as the bomb pot's (`dealOrderOf`), the seal keeps the call it was made under (`Sealed.bombPot`), the rules cannot change while a seal holds, so a call made after the seal applies from the next hand not yet sealed, and nothing after the seal turns the hand into a bomb pot or back. The view says `bombPot.called` and `bombPot.next` and marks the hand in play `hand.bombPot`. A double-board bomb pot (`boardCount` 2) keeps its second board in the hand (`Hand.otherBoards`, read with `boardsOf` and `isDoubleBoard`), deals every street to the first board and then the second, the flop included (two `StreetDealt` events, runs 1 and 2, so the record, the views, the replays, and the felt carry the two boards the way they carry runs, and `HandStarted.bombPot.boardCount` says it is a double board), splits every pot between the boards with `splitEvenly`, the odd chip to the first board, and gives each half to the best hand on its board through `rankHand`, so a double-board Omaha bomb pot is the same rule with the Omaha evaluator; a seat that wins both boards scoops. A double board runs each board once and asks nobody for runs, because the pot is already split two ways, which is what running it twice is for, and four boards would not fit the felt: an all-in run-out deals the rest of both boards street by street after `RunsAgreed { runs: 1 }`. Its showdown follows the `showdown` setting across both boards: automatic shows by `shownAcrossRuns`, starting from the river aggressor, and interactive asks each seat in order as a single board does, where a seat may muck only when its hand neither beats nor ties the tabled hands of a pot it contests on either board (`standingOf` in `src/showing-down.ts` ranks every board, `HandTabled.rank` is the first board's rank), and `awardShowdown` splits each pot between the boards and gives each half to the best tabled claimants on that board. A double board that ends before the river can be rabbit hunted under the same rules as one board: `undealt` is the next cards of the deck, street by street and board by board, two a street, which is the order they would have been dealt, and `rabbitByBoard` in `packages/room/src/witness.ts` splits the hunted cards back onto their boards for the felt. A device that only saw the view reads a double board's draw order street by street, the rabbit after it (`drawOrder` in `packages/room/src/witness.ts`, from the view's `doubleBoard`), and `packages/room/test/fairness.test.ts` checks that every device, live or back from away, checks a double-board hand in time, and that a double board hunted after a fold on the flop checks live and from the view of a device that missed the hunt. PokerKit has no bomb pot, but the replay builds one from PokerKit's own parts (the same ante from every seat with ante trimming, no blinds, and a first street that deals the hole cards and the flop before its betting round) and compares those hands in full; that comparison is what showed the flop has to be dealt before the runs are chosen. - The seven-deuce bounty across the packages: a house rule in the table rules, `sevenDeuce`, `NoBounty` by default or `Bounty` with an amount in chips, the unit of the blinds, the ante, and the buy-in, so no amount is ever multiplied and the table reads it as it reads every other figure. `packages/engine/src/bounty.ts` owns it. Seven-deuce is a seven and a deuce of any suits (`isSevenDeuce`, which the `SevenDeuce` moment uses too, so the table never calls out a seven-deuce the bounty would not count). A seat is owed the bounty when it wins, alone, every pot it was in on every run of the board: everyone folding to it, a showdown, and a short stack that takes the main pot alone while others contest a side pot all count; a split pot, a side pot won while the main pot is lost, and a board run more than once with a run lost do not. Every seat still in is in the main pot, so at most one seat is owed. The engine pays in the same decision that ends the hand, straight after `HandEnded`: `CardsShown` for the cards the table has not seen (a winner by a fold-out, or one nobody called at an interactive showdown, is shown by the rule, the way a showdown tables a winning hand), then `BountyPaid { seat, payers }`, which takes the amount from every other seat dealt into the hand, folded seats included, or all of a stack that holds less; a seat with no chips pays nothing, and when no seat can pay there is no show and no event. A voluntary show after the hand claims nothing, so nothing can arrive once the next hand is sealed, and nobody can stand up between the hand and the show. The room runs its end-of-hand queue after that decision: a seat that is leaving or was removed pays before it cashes out, and chips queued for the end of the hand arrive after. The rules in force when the hand was dealt decide, a hand cancelled by ending a stalled night pays nothing, and a seat sitting out was not dealt in and does not pay. A bounty moves chips between stacks: it is not a pot and not a ledger entry, and every net includes it because a net is cash-outs minus buy-ins. The moment differs from the bounty in one way: it calls out any pot winner who showed seven-deuce, a split or a side pot included, and carries the chips collected as `bounty`, 0 when none was owed. `packages/engine/test/bounty.test.ts` recomputes every payment from the public events and the stacks, `scripts/simulate.ts` counts bounties and deals seven-deuces on purpose, and the PokerKit replay picks the seat from PokerKit's own pots and winners. `packages/room/test/log-ledger.ts` keeps a ledger from the log alone, and `test/bounty.test.ts` checks it against every stack after every event, every cash-out, and every net in the settlement over generated nights, with examples for a leaver, a removed member, a queued top-up, and a cancelled hand. ## Commands

- `pnpm install`: install the workspace. `prepare` patches TypeScript and installs the git hooks.
- `pnpm dev`: run the room server on local workerd at `http://localhost:1337`, or the next free port when that one is taken, and the web app at `http://localhost:5173`. `apps/web/scripts/dev.ts` starts the server, reads the address it reports as ready, and points Vite's `/rooms` proxy there through `ROOM_SERVER_URL`. The server allows the origin in `ALLOWED_ORIGINS`, `http://localhost:5173` by default. It reads no Cloudflare credentials. `scripts/local.run.ts` keeps Alchemy's state in `.alchemy/state`, so a room survives restarting the room server.
- `pnpm check`: lockfile check, strict typecheck, type-aware lint, and format check
- `pnpm test`: build each package and run every Vitest suite once. Turbo builds a package before its tests (`test` depends on `build`), so no package has a `pretest` that builds: one rebuilt `dist` while the web build was reading it and failed CI. Run one package's tests with `pnpm turbo run test --filter=<name>`, not `pnpm --filter <name> test`, which builds nothing
- `pnpm build`: compile each package to `dist`
- `pnpm typecheck`: strict effect-tsrs over every workspace and the root scripts
- `pnpm typecheck:tsc`: patched `tsc`. Run it when a checker pin changes.
- `pnpm typecheck:fast`: tsrs in seconds, without Effect diagnostics. `pnpm check` stays the gate.
- `pnpm lint`: type-aware lint and format check only
- `pnpm fix`: apply Oxfmt and safe Oxlint fixes. The fix deletes comment prose, so move what a comment knows into a name or a test first.
- `pnpm debt`: list every lint, Effect, and TypeScript directive with its reason. It exits non-zero while any directive exists. The list only shrinks.
- `pnpm verify`: run the long-running verification scripts of every package
- `pnpm verify:status` (`apps/room-server/scripts/verify-status.ts`): print, for the last ten commits on `main`, the outcome of the `CI` workflow's push run (check, test, and build) and of each Verify job (ok, FAIL, cancelled, running, queued, or never ran, which means a newer push replaced it in the queue), in a table about 100 columns wide, and then the newest finished result of CI and of every Verify job with its run id. It exits non-zero while the newest finished CI run, or the newest finished result of any Verify job, is a failure. Run it after you push instead of reading `gh run list`, where a replaced run and a failed one look alike.
- `pnpm smoke <base-url>`: check that `/`, a room link, and the preview clip send the same security headers as `/privacy`, then play one hand against a running site, local or deployed. It asks again every 5 seconds, for up to 3 minutes, while opening the first room answers `RoomUnavailable`. CI runs it against the production Worker on local workerd.

### Verification scripts

A long-running check, such as an exhaustive enumeration or a simulator, is a script, not a test.

- Put it in `packages/<name>/scripts/<script>.ts`. The package tsconfig already includes `scripts/`, so the fence checks it.
- Add `"verify": "node scripts/<script>.ts"` to the package `scripts`. Node strips the types. Chain several scripts with `&&`.
- Import the package by its own name, for example `@nostakes/engine`. The name resolves to the built `dist`, and turbo's `verify` task builds the package first.
- Print with Effect `Console`, run the program with `Effect.runPromise`, and fail the Effect on a counterexample. A failed Effect exits non-zero.
- `verify` is not part of `check` or `test`, and turbo never caches it. Run `pnpm verify` before you commit a change to the rules a script covers.
- `apps/room-server` is the exception. Its probes need a deployed Worker, and only the `@alchemy.run/test` harness deploys one to local workerd, so its `verify` runs `scripts/*.verify.ts` through `vitest.verify.config.ts`. Each probe fails its Effect with a tagged error on a counterexample. `scripts/soak.verify.ts` plays at least 1,000 hands across at least 10 rooms, seven in ten of them checked, and its summary counts the bots' fair-shuffle verdicts by kind and the wait beyond the pause before each sealed deal. Set `SOAK_SEED`, `SOAK_HANDS`, and `SOAK_ROOMS` to replay or resize it. Each room's recipe names how its night ends. A mid-hand, stalled, or halted ending first seats the table (`NightControl.stagingTheTable`): the bots stop leaving, standing up, sitting out, napping, and dropping lines, come back and ask for seats at once, and the host bot grants every request, and the supervisor goes on only when its view shows the game running, two seats held by connected bots that can be dealt in, and a deal due; a night that cannot get there in 30 s fails saying which of those was missing, because a jam can only stall a room that is trying to deal. A visitor the room made host hands it to a bot. The endings: as it comes, mid-hand (a bot whose turn it is holds its action, the host sends the close while that bot holds it, and the bot acts once its view shows the close queued, so the close lands in a hand that cannot end first unless that bot's whole clock runs out; if no bot gets a turn within 15 s the host closes as it comes), or stalled (the supervisor jams the room's deals through `/test/rooms/:code/jam`, which `test/fixtures/jammed-room.ts` turns into a `SealedSeatsMoved` refusal of `Deal` and `DealSealed`, waits for the server to mark the room stalled, and then the host or any other bot ends the night). The poker test room backs off fast (`POKER_TEST_RETRIES`, stalled after four refusals in under two seconds). The checks count nights ended between hands, after the hand in play, and from a stalled room, allow refused alarm runs and a seal withdrawn after its round only in a night jammed to stall, and fail a jammed night that was not stalled when it closed and a night that stalled with nobody jamming it. Rooms play in waves of `SOAK_CONCURRENCY` (4 by default), dealt round-robin so each wave mixes small and large tables, and each wave is judged after it stops playing and before the next one starts. Judging a room is synchronous and would freeze rooms still playing, and keeping every room's frames until the end makes memory grow with the number of rooms, so neither may come back. Each room prints its outcome as soon as its wave is judged, and the summary prints the peak heap, the event-loop stalls while rooms played, and the funnel and health counts the rooms recorded, read by the same `scripts/funnel.ts` that `pnpm stats` uses. While rooms play it prints the live heap every minute with what each room holds (frames by kind and their text, and timeline notes), and again after each wave is judged. At `SOAK_HEAP_LIMIT_MB` (4096 by default, half the 8 GB heap `vitest.verify.config.ts` gives the run, and confirmed after a forced GC) it stops with `SoakHeapLimit`, naming the rooms and what they hold, and still prints its summary, instead of dying out of memory. `SOAK_ONLY=3,7,11` plays only those rooms of the plan, to replay one wave. A bot keeps each frame it received as its text (`test/fixtures/held-arrival.ts`) with only the 32 most recent decoded per socket and none once the socket closes, and the judge keeps a digest and the cards of each received event and view instead of the parsed frame, decoding each frame once for both the frame checks and the deck checks. The harness's memory therefore grows with the frame text alone, about 5 KB a frame (a nine-seat night of 85 hands holds about 1.3 GB), and every night fails if the harness kept a decoded frame of a closed socket. Every bot sends a device id, and rooms whose seeds share a remainder by four share devices. The soak stops itself after `SOAK_BUDGET_MINUTES` (95 by default, below the test's own limit) and still prints its summary, so a run that is too slow says what it saw instead of being killed silently. On every push to `main`, Verify plays a shorter soak with the other room-server probes: the same 12 room shapes with 160 hands, 6 at a time, within 20 minutes. The full 1,000 hands run as the `room-server full soak` job, 3 rooms at a time, every night and on manual dispatch. A failed room prints its seed and code and saves its stored log to a temporary directory, with a timeline beside it: the night's own steps and, for each client bot, its connection phases, its lines, every frame it sent, swallowed, or got, and each command with its outcome. Set `SOAK_KEEP_LOGS=true` to save every room's log and timeline. `scripts/round-trip.verify.ts` times 1,000 commands, each sent by one of four players at no more than the command rate, until all three others see it. `scripts/hibernation.verify.ts` pings through a 30-second idle and checks the room still hibernates. `scripts/silence.verify.ts` waits about 60 seconds for a quiet socket to be closed as `Unresponsive` while a pinging one stays.

## Fence

The fence makes cheating uncomfortable. If a hook fails, fix the failure. Do not disable the fence.

- Lefthook pre-commit runs `pnpm check` and `pnpm test`.
- Every vitest config spreads `testBudget` from `tools/fence/budget/config.ts`. Its setup file measures each test's CPU time and fails a test that spends more than a third of its own limit (`CPU_SHARE_OF_LIMIT`), with a message that says to make it cheaper rather than raise the limit. CPU time barely moves when the runner is busy, so a contended runner does not trip it; a test that trips it would time out on a slow one. Its reporter prints the tests that used the most of their limit after every run, in CI too.
- Never skip the hooks: no `--no-verify`, no `commit -n`, no `LEFTHOOK=0`, no `core.hooksPath` override. The Claude Code hook in `.claude/settings.json` denies these commands. The policy lives in `scripts/vcs-command-policy.js`.
- `tsconfig.base.json` owns the Effect diagnostic severities. A package tsconfig may add an exact `allowedUnstableApis` entry only for a module the checker warns about. `engine` and `ledger` already allow `@effect/vitest` and `effect/Arbitrary`, because every test needs them. `apps/room-server` also allows the `effect/http` modules it uses, and `effect/FileSystem` and `@effect/platform-node/NodeFileSystem` for the soak's saved logs.
- Some rules collide with common Effect forms. Use the form that passes both:
  - `unicorn/no-array-method-this-argument` reads any two-argument `.map`, `.filter`, or `.forEach` as an array call with `thisArg`. `Option.map(option, f)` and `Effect.forEach(items, f)` trip it. Write `option.pipe(Option.map(f))` and `pipe(items, Effect.forEach(f))`. `Effect.forEach(f, { concurrency })` trips it too, so write `Effect.all(items.map(f), { concurrency })`.
  - `anti-slop-effect/no-service-constructor-imports` rejects importing any `make<Name>` outside a test file. Name a factory for what it returns, such as `hostRoom` or `roomObject`.
  - `no-empty-object-type` rejects an empty `Data.TaggedEnum` variant. Give every variant a field that says something.
  - `anti-slop-effect/no-manual-tagged-construction` rejects a `_tag` in an object literal, test fixtures included. Compare wire frames as JSON text when a test needs the literal form.
  - `anti-slop-effect/no-manual-tagged-type` rejects a `_tag` in a type or interface, `Extract<U, { _tag: "A" }>` included. Write `Types.ExtractTag<U, "A">`, and derive a variant from a `Schema.TaggedStruct` or a `Data.TaggedEnum`.
  - The Effect diagnostic `missingReturnYieldStar` wants `return yield*` on an Effect that never succeeds, and `consistent-return` rejects a generator that mixes `return;` with `return yield* ...`. Collect the outcome in a variable, leave the loop, and end with one `return yield* Option.match(outcome, { onNone: () => Effect.void, onSome: (problem) => Effect.fail(...) })`.
  - `promise/prefer-await-to-then` reads `Effect.catch` as a promise `.catch`, and `promise/prefer-await-to-callbacks` reads a callback whose parameter is named `error` as a Node callback. Use `Effect.mapError` or `Effect.catchTag`, and name the parameter `failure`.
  - `anti-slop/no-shape-in-symbol-names` rejects any name containing "shape". Name the thing for what it holds, such as `recipe` or `plan`.
  - A room view or rejection holds `Option` values, which plain `JSON.stringify` cannot carry. Wrap a core's schemas in `Schema.toCodecJson` before `Schema.fromJsonString`. `HostedRoom` and `ClientSchemas` take a `WireCodec`, whose encoded side is `Schema.Json`, so a schema that skips this does not typecheck.
  - `typescript/no-unsafe-type-assertion` rejects a narrowing assertion even with a `SAFETY:` comment. Narrow with a type predicate instead, as `src/workerd.ts` does for the globals only workerd defines.
  - A type-level test uses `expectTypeOf` from `@effect/vitest`, not `@ts-expect-error`, because every directive counts as debt.
- `no-redeclare` rejects Foldkit's habit of naming a schema and its type alike (`const Model` beside `type Model`). Name the schema `ModelSchema` and the Message union `Messages`, and keep `Model` and `Message` for the types.
- `xstate-effect/no-inline-effect` rejects an Effect returned from an inline `enq` callback and inline Effect logic passed to `enq.spawn`. Declare Effect work in `setupEffect({ actors })`.
- CI runs `pnpm install --frozen-lockfile` in each of its three jobs, then check and build, the tests of every package but the room server, and the room server's tests (after building its packages), side by side. Locally `turbo.json` holds the room server's tests until the other packages' have run; on CI they run in a job of their own.

## Project law

- Chips are integers. No floating point anywhere near a chip amount. Model amounts with an integer Schema. Never divide chips with `/`, and never repair a result with `Math.round`. Every split names its rule for the remainder.
- Pure domain packages (`engine`, `ledger`) do no I/O and read no clock or randomness. Inputs such as the shuffled deck arrive as data. The Effect diagnostics `globalDate`, `globalRandom`, `globalTimers`, `globalConsole`, `globalFetch`, `cryptoRandomUUID`, and `nodeBuiltinImport` are errors, and `nostakes-patterns/no-module-level-mutable-state` bans module-level `let` and `var`.
- Errors are part of the interface. Define each one as a `Schema.TaggedError`, for example `class SeatTaken extends Schema.TaggedError<SeatTaken>()("SeatTaken", { seat: Schema.Int }) {}`. Name the failure, keep its context, and say how to fix it.
- Schemas are the contract. Parse unknown input with Effect `Schema` at the boundary. Trust internal types after that. Do not validate the same value twice.
- No comments in code. `no-comments/no-comments` bans them. Agents copy comments, and a comment that explains a workaround spreads the workaround. Say it in a name, a type, a test, or the commit message. Three kinds survive:
  - A tool directive with its reason after `--`: `// oxlint-disable-next-line <rule> -- <reason>`, `// @effect-diagnostics-next-line <rule>:off -- <reason>`, or `// @ts-expect-error -- <reason>`
  - A one-line `SAFETY:` comment above a type assertion that names the invariant it relies on
  - A JSDoc block made only of type tags in a plain JS file
- Credit for borrowed code lives in `PROVENANCE.md`. The repository is licensed `AGPL-3.0-only` (`LICENSE`); the owner chose it and holds the copyright. A new dependency or borrowed file must carry a licence that AGPL-3.0 can include (MIT, ISC, BSD, Apache-2.0, MPL-2.0, LGPL, the Open Font License), and anything else needs the owner's yes.
- Tests prove behaviour. Write Effect tests with `it.effect` and `it.layer` from `@effect/vitest`. `Effect.run*` and `ManagedRuntime.make` in a test file fail lint.
- Write a test only when its failure would mean one of these: chips are wrong (hand rules, pots, splits, bounty and bomb pot money, a cancelled hand's returns, the ledger, the settlement); a hidden card or a secret leaks (redaction, replays, moments, knocks, logs, analytics); the deal is unfair or the check lies (seals, the beacon, the deal order, verdicts); a room is lost, stuck, or out of sync (commits, halts, deadlines, ending a night, resume, the wire contract); someone gets in, stays, or injects who should not (passes, removal, the lock, approval, limits, markup, the page policy); or a regression that already happened and cost real time. Do not test wording, copy tables, labels, captions, picture or clip layout, timing pins, UI gesture walk-throughs, what the type checker or an exhaustive `Match` holds, or fixtures and harness code. Keep one end-to-end check per feature whose outcome is the player's data. The avatar art digest and the wire contract are the only change detectors. Say in one line what you did not test and why.
- A generated property runs a quick size in `test` and its full size in `verify`: `test/scale.ts` gives `generated(quick, full)` and `sized(quick, full)`, and `vitest.verify.config.ts` provides `fullScale`. A long check whose value is scale is a `verify` script, not a test.
- Prefer properties and models. A domain rule is an `it.prop` or `it.effect.prop` over Schema or `effect/Arbitrary` inputs. Anything with history, such as a hand or a session, is a model checked over generated command sequences. Keep example tests for seams.
- Plant a bug before you trust a new test. The test must fail, then pass once you restore the code. Tautological tests and change-detector tests are harmful.
- Delete `passWithNoTests` from a package's `vitest.config.ts` when its first test lands.
- Lint baselines only shrink. Add a lint rule before you clean up a bad pattern.

## Architecture

- Dependencies run from `apps` to `packages`. `nostakes-boundaries/no-cross-layer-imports` stops a package from importing app code.
- `engine` owns the rules of a hand. `ledger` owns the money across a session. Neither imports the other yet.
- Pure domain state machines are plain reducers over tagged unions. `decide(state, command)` returns the events the command causes, or fails with a tagged error. `evolve(state, event)` returns the next state. Branch with `Match.tagsExhaustive` or a `Data.taggedEnum` `$match`. A `switch` on `_tag` fails lint (`anti-slop-effect/no-manual-tag-comparison`).
- `protocol` owns the wire, including the close reasons and their codes, the ping and pong texts, the ping interval, and the server's silence limit. `room-server` owns identity, storage, delivery, and limits, and depends on `protocol`. The pure room core plugs into the server through `HostedRoom` in one module and never sees a socket, a token, or a sequence number.
- Lifecycles with side effects are XState machines. This is a deliberate reading of rat-stack's "lifecycles are machines" rule. A pure reducer has no side effect to retry, cancel, or resume, so it needs no actor. A lifecycle that waits, times out, or talks to the network gets a machine.
- The room server has no XState machine. Its only lifecycle is "wait until a deadline, then act", and that deadline is data in the core's state (`nextDeadline`), re-read on every start and after every commit and armed as the Durable Object alarm. There is no hidden state for a machine to own. Every change to a room goes through one synchronous commit function in `src/room-host.ts` that cannot await.
- The client's connection lifecycle has hidden state and side effects, so it is one XState machine, `roomClientMachine` in `packages/client/src/machine.ts`. XState owns the states (Idle, Connecting, Greeting, Live, Waiting, Refused, Outdated, Stopped) and which transitions are legal. Declared Effect actors own the work. The Online state invokes the connection actor, so the socket lives exactly as long as Online. Time, backoff jitter, and the socket are the Effect services `Clock`, `Random`, and `Transport`, each with a live and a test layer. The embedding app sends `wake` when the page becomes visible or the network returns.
- Arm a machine timer as a named delayed event with an id, and cancel it on exit. XState's built-in `xstate.after` and `xstate.done.actor` events are not in a machine's event type, so a pure model test could only fire them through an unsafe cast. For the same reason a child actor relays named events to its parent instead of finishing with output.
- `Effect.sleep` inside a loop fails lint (`nostakes-patterns/no-sleep-in-loop`). Waiting belongs in a machine.

## Source control

- Trunk-based development. Commit small atomic changes straight to `main`. Every commit passes `pnpm check` and `pnpm test` through the hook.
- Use a conventional prefix (`feat:`, `fix:`, `test:`, `build:`, `ci:`, `docs:`, `chore:`) and an imperative subject under 72 characters. Add a body when the why is not obvious.
- Stage only the files your change touched. Never commit `dist`, `.turbo`, or secrets.

## Boundaries and sign-off

- Safe by default: code and tests inside a package, a new or tighter test or lint rule, a targeted override with a written reason, an `allowedUnstableApis` entry the checker asks for, a new verification script, and edits to `AGENTS.md` and `README.md`.
- Routine dependency bumps need no sign-off: a patch or minor release of something already pinned, with no breaking change for this repo, landed through the normal gate with CI and the Verify jobs green, and reported to the owner afterwards. Read the release notes first and keep every pin exact.
- Needs owner sign-off: adding or removing a dependency, a major version, a bump with a breaking change or a migration, and any new patch under `patches/`, because pins are exact and CI installs cold. Any edit that loosens `oxlint.config.ts`, the diagnostics in `tsconfig.base.json`, `lefthook.yml`, `.claude/settings.json`, or `scripts/vcs-command-policy.js`. A new package or app. Anything that brings I/O, a clock, or randomness into `engine` or `ledger`.
