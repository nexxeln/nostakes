# Agent instructions

This file is the repo law for agents and contributors. `VISION.md` says what the product is for. Read it before you design anything. It holds the commands, the validation rules, the architecture constraints, and the stop rules. The fence (types, lint, hooks, CI) enforces most of it. Fence wins over prose.

nostakes is a web app where groups of friends play poker online, at most 9 players per room. Chip accounting must be exactly right.

## Packages

- `@nostakes/engine` (`packages/engine`): pure no-limit hold'em rules
- `@nostakes/ledger` (`packages/ledger`): buy-ins, cash-outs, and settle-up transfers
- `@nostakes/room` (`packages/room`): one poker night as a pure reducer over the engine, the ledger, and the shuffle: members, host, seats, clocks, the queue at the end of a hand, views, and redaction. A member who leaves the room keeps a working pass and a per-person settlement. The view lists them in `departed` with their name and face until they join again, marks their seat `leavingRoom` while it plays out the hand, and still names them in `you` on their own device. The room takes `RoomCodeSchema` from `@nostakes/protocol`, the one source for a room code's format. In a room whose `shuffle` setting is `Checked` (the default for a new room), `src/sealing.ts` seals each hand (`SealDue`, `SealHand`, `HandSealed`) and deals it from the sealed beacon round (`SealedDealDue`, `DealSealed`), verifying the round's signature against the chain in `RoomOpened`; `src/openings.ts` sends every shown card's opening to exactly its audience; `src/witness.ts` turns what one device saw into `judgeHand`'s input. Once a seal binds, the sealed seats are frozen until the hand is dealt, and the end-of-hand queue never runs in a batch that deals hole cards (`QueueDue`, `RunQueue`). A sealed hand is dealt with the posting seats and the table rules its seal used: a posting choice made after the seal, or a rules change (queued while a hand is sealed), applies from the next hand. `test/deadlines.test.ts` checks that the room accepts the server's answer to every deadline it names, because a refused one stops the game for good. Ending the night (`CloseRoom`, `src/closing.ts`) closes at once between hands. During a hand, or while a sealed hand is bound to be dealt, it is queued and the hand finishes on its clocks; the host can act for any seat. Once the server has marked the room stalled (`NoteStall`, `TableStalled`, cleared by `StallCleared` when the room next accepts a deadline), any member may end the night and it closes at once: a hand in play is cancelled with `HandCancelled`, which gives every dealt-in seat back the stack it held when the hand was dealt, so every chip in the pot, blinds and dead money included, goes back to the seat that put it in, chips queued for the end of the hand are dropped, and a binding seal is withdrawn. The view's `closing` (`Now`, `AfterHand`, `CancelsHand`) says which of these ending the night does at that moment. Attendance also notes the host: `HostAway` when the host has no live socket and `HostBack` when they return, and any other member may `ClaimHost` once the host has been away for `HOST_CLAIM_AFTER_SECONDS` (120), which the view shows as `hostAway.claimableAt`, so a night whose host vanished can still be ended and settled. `test/ending.test.ts` ends sampled states of generated nights, including planted sealed deals the room refuses and hands whose deadlines it refuses, within 400 commands from the host, and checks the settlement against the buy-ins and cash-outs in the log and every cancelled hand's returns against the stacks when it was dealt. A checked hand costs about 18 ms of BLS checking in `decide`, so the generic room properties make one room in twenty checked. `packages/shuffle/DESIGN.md` section 12 records the design as built. `src/signals.ts` names the signals a room relays outside its log, `RoomSignalSchema`: `Peeking` on or off, and a `Reaction` from the fixed set in `REACTIONS`. `signalAllowed` says who may send one: a peek only from a member whose seat holds hole cards in the hand in progress, and only while the `peeking` setting is on; a reaction from any member, seated or watching.
- `@nostakes/shuffle` (`packages/shuffle`): the provably fair shuffle, pure, used by the room for checked hands. It seals two decks per hand as Merkle trees of salted cards, orders the deal from a drand quicknet beacon round named in the seal, checks drand's BLS signature, judges from a device's own clock whether the seal arrived before its round, lists sealed hands with no deal, and gives one verdict per hand (`judgeHand`). `DESIGN.md` there holds the threat model, the margins, and the cheat table.
- `@nostakes/avatar` (`packages/avatar`): the players' faces, pure, in the House style: every face is a stack of paper sheets cut from polygons with eased corners. An avatar is a Schema of parts (species, colour, suit, card, eyes, mouth, headwear, eyewear, neck, mark, extra) that encodes to a 13-character `h1` code inside the room's 512-character avatar string (`codeFor`, `AvatarFromCode`). The cast is eight species: great white, hammerhead, goldfish, puffer, piranha, angler, whale, and octopus. Each wears a card with the avatar's rank and suit (a shark's fin, a goldfish's crest, the angler's bait), and its pupils, pendant, and fold are that suit's pips. `avatarFor` reads any stored string: an `s1` code from the shark art keeps its species and wardrobe, and anything else, including an old `c1` code, an emoji, or garbage, gets `randomAvatar(seedFrom(text))`, the same face every time. `render(avatar, { expression, size, theme, layers })` returns an SVG string for six table expressions: `Resting`, `LookingDown`, `Thinking`, `Happy`, `Gutted`, and `Folded`. `theme` (`Light` by default) picks the ground colours the cuts and shadows are drawn for, and `layers` wraps each sheet in `<g class="sheet" style="--sheet:N">` so the face builder can pull the stack apart. Every lifted sheet in `src/sheets.ts` is four paths: one flat offset shadow whose depth grows with the sheet's place in the stack (fins 0.9, body 1, white card 1.35, ink 1.7, gold 1.9, all times 1.15), a hairline gap in the ground colour, a light top edge, and the fill, each sheet turned a little off true. A shadow never falls below 1.4 screen pixels, and below 56 px it darkens into an offset edge at 1.5 px, so the stack still reads at 40 px. The output has no ids, `url(#)`, filters, or blend modes, so nine faces share a page cheaply; a fold is cut geometrically (`clipBelow`, `foldDown`) instead of with a clip path. The shapes, the sheet order, the palette, and the shadow rule are ported from the House design scripts; the art is original and drawn in code.
- `@nostakes/protocol` (`packages/protocol`): the JSON frames between a room's clients and its server, the room pass, and the wire limits. Beside commands and events it carries signals, such as a peek or a reaction: a client sends `Signal`, and the server relays it to the room's other live sockets as `Signal { from, signal }`. A signal is never stored, sequenced, replayed on reconnect, or answered, and losing one breaks nothing. `SIGNALS_PER_SECOND` and `SIGNAL_BURST` bound each player's signals. It is generic over a room core's schemas, so it never imports the room, and it does no I/O. `PROTOCOL_VERSION` in `src/version.ts` names the wire. The client sends it in `Hello`, and a server on another version closes the socket with `ClientOutdated` or `ServerOutdated`, which every version from 1 reads. A hello that names no version predates versions and is closed as `ProtocolViolation`. `Welcome.build` names the web build the server serves. Bump `PROTOCOL_VERSION` whenever a frame or a room schema changes. `apps/room-server/test/wire-contract.test.ts` digests the encoded JSON Schema of every frame, the room's command, event, view, and rejection, and the HTTP bodies, and fails until the new version's digests are appended to `test/wire-contract.ts`. Never edit an entry already recorded.
- `@nostakes/client` (`packages/client`): the headless room client. It holds one connection, resumes after a drop without missing or repeating an event, resends unanswered commands with their ids, and exposes the view, the applied events, the connection state, and presence. Its `updates` stream gives each view together with the events that produced it and the sequence number it reflects, in the order the room applied them, so a UI never shows a view ahead of the events that led to it. The web app takes its view and its events only from `updates`. `signal` sends a signal at once while the client is live and otherwise drops it (`NotLive`); it is never queued, resent, or owed an answer, and past the device's own `SIGNAL_ALLOWANCE` it is dropped there (`OverLimit`), so a busy UI cannot crowd commands out of the socket's frame budget. `signals` streams each signal the room relays with its sender, apart from `updates`. Like `protocol` it is generic over a core's schemas and never imports the room. It reads no `window` or `document`. While live it is owed a `Pong` for each `Ping` and a `Reply` for each `Command`. The connection tells the machine as each such frame leaves the throttle, and the machine arms one deadline per frame, `ANSWER_LIMIT_MS` from that moment. A ping whose pong is late marks the line overdue and starts a grace of `OVERDUE_GRACE_MS`, counted from when the machine notices, so a pong that reached the socket while the app was busy still counts. If it is still unanswered when the grace ends, the client drops the socket as `Unanswered`. A late reply is not proof that nobody hears the client, because a slow room delays replies while the runtime still answers pings, so a late reply sends one ping probe and lists the command in `waitingForTable` instead of dropping. The probe's own pong deadline decides whether the line is heard. A command still unanswered `REPLY_LAST_RESORT_MS` after it left drops the connection once, in case the socket is half-broken in a way pings do not show. Its unit and model tests use a fake socket and the test clock. `test/long-night.test.ts` applies 6,000 batches and fails if the client keeps more than 2 kB of memory per batch. Its `verify` (`scripts/long-night.ts`) applies 20,000, prints the CPU and the heap for each 1,000, and fails on the same memory limit, on CPU that grows more than three times, or on a last batch not applied. Its tests against the real server live in `apps/room-server/test/client-room.test.ts`, because a package may not import app code.
- `@nostakes/room-server` (`apps/room-server`): the Cloudflare Worker and one Durable Object per room, declared with Alchemy. It hosts any core that satisfies `HostedRoom` in `src/hosted-room.ts`. `src/poker-room.ts` adapts `@nostakes/room` to that port for one drand chain (`pokerRoomOn`), and `src/poker-room-object.ts` is the production object. Before a sealed deal the alarm asks the `Beacon` service in `src/beacon.ts` for the round's signature: the live layer asks drand's relays until 15 s after the round was due, and the test layer (`test/fixtures/test-beacon.ts`) signs rounds with its own key inside workerd, is down on every 29th round, and never touches the network. `src/serve-rooms.ts` is the Worker body. `src/worker.ts` wraps it for local development and tests, with allowed origins read from the `ALLOWED_ORIGINS` config. `src/site-worker.ts` wraps it for nostakes.club: one Worker serves the built web app as static assets and runs the room routes first for `/rooms` and `/rooms/*`, so the page and the server share one origin, the stage's own. It also runs first for `/`, `/r/*`, and `/preview/*` through `src/site-pages.ts`, because chat apps read link previews from the HTML without running the app: `/` serves `index.html` and a room link serves `invite.html`, each with `https://nostakes.club` in its tags replaced by the request's own origin, and `/preview/*` answers byte ranges with 206, which the asset layer does not and iOS needs to play the preview clip. `scripts/production.run.ts` declares that Worker with Alchemy for the stages `production` (nostakes.club) and `preview` (preview.nostakes.club), with Alchemy's state in a Cloudflare state store named `nostakes-state`. Cloudflare rate limits in `src/address-limits.ts` cap rooms, joins, and socket upgrades per address. A room deletes its storage `ROOM_RETENTION_MS`, 30 days, after its last event. Its tests drive a toy core and the real room through real WebSocket clients on local workerd. Seeded bots in `test/fixtures/bot.ts` play whole nights, and `test/fixtures/night-checks.ts` checks every frame they received against the stored log. Bots of both kinds send signals as they play (`test/fixtures/signal-plan.ts`): peeks while holding cards, peeks after folding or while watching that the room must drop, and reactions. The checks place each signal a socket bot received at the sequence number that socket had reached, which is the state the server judged it in, and fail the night on any signal the sender's own view at that point does not allow. Every bot device also judges every hand it saw with `judgeHand` (`test/fixtures/fair-checks.ts`), checks a "beacon unavailable" deal against the test relay itself, and fails the night on a failed verdict, a card shown without its opening, or a sealed hand never dealt. Each night splits its bots by seed into raw socket bots, which also break the protocol on purpose, and client bots in `test/fixtures/client-bot.ts`, which run `@nostakes/client` over the live WebSocket transport and play from its view. A client bot's wire (`test/fixtures/client-wire.ts`) can be closed from the bot's side, black-holed, deafened, taken offline, or fed a batch it already applied. A line only gets worse: a deaf line can be black-holed, but a black-holed line never hears again, because a real socket cannot lose frames and then deliver later ones. A night ends deterministically: once the room starts closing, each client bot closes a black-holed line, even while it waits for a reply, and leaves a deaf line for the client to drop as `Unanswered`. Some client bots deafen their line as the room closes to prove the client heals it alone. The night stops only when every client bot is live, has nothing pending, and sees the room closed. A client bot that cannot get there within `SETTLE_LIMIT_MS` fails the night. The checks trace every accepted client command to the batch in the log that `decide` reproduces for it, account for every sequence number in the client's activity, and compare the client's view at the end of each hand with `viewFor`, apart from clocks. A live batch is redacted for each viewer's audience after the batch. A catch-up redacts each missed event for the audience right after that event, from the states the host keeps for the last 201 events, because a seat can change hands between batches. `Welcome.catchUp` says what it covers: `UpToDate`, `Missed { from, events }`, or `Skipped { from, to }`, and the client checks the range instead of inferring it. A browser never sees the HTTP status of a refused upgrade, so a socket for a wrong code, a halted room, or a full room is accepted and closed with a `Closing` frame (`src/refused-socket.ts`). Only a wrong origin gets an HTTP refusal. A call from the Worker to a room can fail on Cloudflare, which refuses calls to a new Durable Object namespace for a while after its first deploy. That failure is a `RoomCallFailed` (`src/room-call-failed.ts`), logged with the room code and Cloudflare's reference and answered 503 `RoomUnavailable`, which a client may retry. Any other defect in a room route is logged as a `RouteFault` and answered 500 `ServerFault`, never an empty body. Each start of a room object logs one line with its instance and how many sockets it found, so the Worker's logs show when a room hibernated and woke. Every close the server makes goes through `closeWith` in `src/socket-close.ts`, and `test/close-paths.test.ts` fails on any other socket close. The object registers the exact ping and pong texts with the hibernation auto-response, so a ping never wakes a room, and it closes a live socket it has not heard from for `SOCKET_SILENCE_LIMIT_MS` with `Unresponsive`. After each commit the host counts the stored rows of the batch and halts if one is missing. When the room refuses the server's own answer to its next deadline, the host (`answerDeadline` in `src/room-host.ts`) logs a `RefusedDeadline` with the room code, the deadline's kind, and the rejection, and waits before it answers that deadline again: 1 s, doubling to a cap of 60 s (`RETRY_POLICY` in `src/refused-deadline.ts`), kept in the room's `refusals` table so a hibernation or restart does not reset it. An answer a member's command made stale is not counted. At the fifth refusal in a row of the same deadline it commits the core's `stalled` command, which the poker room records as `TableStalled` and shows in every view as `stalled`; the room clears it with `StallCleared` when it next accepts any deadline. A stalled room is never halted, because a halted room refuses everything and would trap the chips. The toy core's `Jam` switch refuses its own deadlines, and its object uses a fast policy (`TOY_RETRIES`). The night checks fail a night in which any alarm run's own command was refused, and the soak prints that count for a night its watchdog stopped. A core names its signals in `schemas.signal` and says who may send one with `signalAllowed`. The object relays an allowed signal from a live socket to every other live socket in the room at once, with no storage write and no alarm, and silently drops one from a socket that has not said hello, one the core refuses, and one over the sender's per-player allowance (`SIGNAL_ALLOWANCE` from `@nostakes/protocol`), which never closes the socket. A signal reaching a hibernated room wakes it like any frame, and the object replays its log once before relaying; `scripts/signal-wake.verify.ts` checks that a wave wakes the room once, reaches a hibernated socket, stores nothing, and lets the room hibernate again. Compare sockets by reference: Effect's `Equal` finds two workerd sockets structurally equal, so `Option.contains` on a socket matches every socket.
- Version and attendance across the packages: the web build stamps a content id into `index.html` and `build.json` (`vite.config.ts`), and `pnpm deploy:<stage>` passes it to the Worker as `WEB_BUILD`, which the room object reads and sends in each `Welcome`. The client ends in `Outdated` when the server calls it outdated, and reports `newBuild` while live when the server serves another build. The page reloads at once for a newer protocol and after the hand the player is in for a new build, records the reload in `sessionStorage`, and stops with a plain message instead of reloading again for the same version (`src/features/page-update.ts`). The room object asks `HostedRoom.attendance` after every frame, socket close, alarm, and start, with the players who have a live socket. The room core turns that into `NoteAttendance`, records `TableUnattended` when no seated player is connected (a spectator does not count), and once `pauseWhenAwaySeconds` (default 120) has run out before the next deal, `QuietDue` and `QuietTable` record `TableWentQuiet`: the hand in play finishes on its clocks and no new hand is dealt, so the log stops growing and the room ages toward deletion. `TableAttended` resumes the game when a seated player is back.
- `@nostakes/web` (`apps/web`): the browser app, a Foldkit program built with Vite. It talks to the server only through `@nostakes/client` and the three HTTP routes, and never imports `apps/room-server`. In development Vite proxies `/rooms` to the local room server, so the page and the server share one origin. Browser globals live in `src/client`; `src/features` holds the Model, the Messages, the pure update, and the views. The create and join screens build a player's face with `src/features/face-builder.ts`, and the room header reopens it as a sheet that sends `SetProfile`. The profile keeps the avatar as its code, and the device remembers it for the next room. A refusal reaches the update as the room's structured rejection, and `src/features/rejections.ts` turns it into a sentence with names from the current view. Its match is exhaustive over the room's and the table's rejections, and `test/rejections.test.ts` renders every rejection type with marked player ids, so a new rejection without a sentence, or a sentence that shows a raw id, fails. A view's clocks carry `at`, the server time the view was computed at, and each clock's `deadline` and full length `totalMs` in server time. The page keeps the differences between `at` and the local time of the last 8 updates, and counts each ring down to its `deadline` from the local time plus the largest difference, which comes from the quickest update. A late or repeated copy of a view therefore gives back no time. The tolerance is that quickest update's one-way delay, usually well under a second: a ring may end that much after the server's deadline, and never before it. While a player holds, hovers, or presses Space over their cards, the table tells the room `Peeking` and repeats it every `PEEK_REFRESH_MS` (`src/features/signals.ts`); every other device turns that face `LookingDown` while the seat still holds cards and the room shows peeks, for at most `PEEK_LEASE_MS` after the latest one, so a lost stop cannot leave a face stuck looking down. A tap on your own face, or `E` on a keyboard, opens a row of the room's `REACTIONS`; a tap or a digit sends one, and it rises from the sender's face, or from the middle for someone not seated, and fades, in place under reduced motion. Every colour, type size, spacing step, and radius lives in `src/client/tokens.css`, in the House look: ink on warm paper, one state colour (vermilion) for whose turn it is, the pot, the action, and a win, and every surface a paper sheet with eased corners, a light top edge, and one flat offset shadow with no blur (`--shadow-sheet-0` to `--shadow-sheet-3`, pressed down by `--press-1` to `--press-3`). Text is Figtree and display type DM Sans, both under the SIL Open Font License, subset and served from `src/client/fonts` (credits in `PROVENANCE.md`). The menu (`src/features/menu-view.ts`) is one page ordered by how often each thing is used: your seat, the host's Deal and Pause, the house rules as a card in poker shorthand, everyone in the room with their net for the night (the host taps a person to act for them, hand over the host, or remove them), and the device toggles. Ending the night, leaving, and removing a player ask once in a sheet that says what happens. The house rules (`src/features/rules-view.ts`) open from the card for everyone and are editable by the host: nine groups by the moment a rule affects, one line per rule with a switch, segments, or a stepper, help only when a label is tapped, rules that do not matter under another rule hidden, and four presets that set the style of game. `src/features/rules-copy.ts` is the one table from each setting to its label, help, group, and control, and `test/rules.test.ts` fails on a setting with no entry, an entry for no setting, or a control that does not offer exactly the setting's values; a setting without an entry still shows under Other with its schema title. Never word a rule in the room's schema annotations for the screen: they are part of the wire contract. The menu's device toggles also switch the card faces between two and four colours, remembered as `nostakes.deck` and applied as `data-deck` on the page root, never sent to the room. Every face is drawn for the page's theme (`pageTheme` in `src/features/model.ts`). A seat that won a pot carries `data-awarded="+N"` until the next hand, and `scripts/two-players.ts` reads that, not a style class. So do the safe-area insets (`--inset-*`) and the gutters that clear them. The home and invitation screens show your face as one row you tap to open the face sheet, so the primary action fits on a 360 by 640 screen. `runLanes` in `src/client/table/layout.ts` places the extra boards of a hand run more than once where they cover no face, your cards, or the chip, and `test/table-layout.test.ts` checks that across phone and tablet sizes. Key hints show only with a fine pointer and no touch screen. The home screen lists up to three rooms this device holds a pass for, newest first, with who else was there and whether the room is open, settled, or left, so a closed tab is one tap from your seat. The device keeps that record (`nostakes.rooms`) from the views it sees, and checks each listed room with a socket that sends no Hello (`src/client/room-check.ts`): a deleted room answers `Closing NotFound` and is forgotten, while a live room only waits, so nobody sees you arrive. After each hand the device checks the deck itself: `src/client/fairness.ts`, loaded only once a room connects, folds every update into the room's witness (an update counts as live only when the connection was already live, the page visible, and it is not the first update after a reconnect), judges each finished hand with `judgeHand` when the page is idle, fetches a "beacon unavailable" round from drand's relays to test the claim, and reports sealed hands never dealt. The room header shows one small mark for the latest hand, or an alarm while any kept hand failed, and the sheet behind it gives one paragraph per hand from `src/features/fairness-text.ts`, with the proof to copy when a check failed. `scripts/two-players.ts` fails if the first hand's mark is an alarm. The first load holds only the app shell. The room client with XState (`src/client/room-link.ts`), the table element, and the deck check load as their own chunks when a room needs them, and the table adopts a snapshot or sound setting made before its definition arrived. The build treats every workspace package's `dist` and `@noble` as free of side effects (`PURE_MODULES` in `vite.config.ts`), so modules the app never calls, such as the room's own `decide` and the BLS check, stay out of the first load; a package module must not rely on being run for its side effects. The page holds a screen wake lock while you sit in a running game (`keepsScreenAwake` in `src/features/room.ts`), and `ApplyTheme` repaints the `theme-color` tags so the browser's chrome matches a chosen theme. The link-preview tags come from `src/features/link-preview.ts`: a Vite plugin writes them into `index.html` and emits `invite.html` with the invitation text. `pnpm --filter @nostakes/web brand` regenerates `public/`: the favicon, the app icons, the preview card (`preview/card.png`, also the clip's poster), and the preview clip (`preview/clip.mp4`), which it records from the real table element in `scripts/brand/stage.html` in headless, muted Chromium and encodes with `ffmpeg`. Run it when the avatar art or the table's look changes, and commit what it writes.
- `@nostakes/fence` (`tools/fence`): fixture tests for the lint rules and the hook policy. It ships nothing.

## Commands

- `pnpm install`: install the workspace. `prepare` patches TypeScript and installs the git hooks.
- `pnpm dev`: run the room server on local workerd at `http://localhost:1337`, or the next free port when that one is taken, and the web app at `http://localhost:5173`. `apps/web/scripts/dev.ts` starts the server, reads the address it reports as ready, and points Vite's `/rooms` proxy there through `ROOM_SERVER_URL`. The server allows the origin in `ALLOWED_ORIGINS`, `http://localhost:5173` by default. It reads no Cloudflare credentials. `scripts/local.run.ts` keeps Alchemy's state in `.alchemy/state`, so a room survives restarting the room server.
- `pnpm check`: lockfile check, strict typecheck, type-aware lint, and format check
- `pnpm test`: build each package and run every Vitest suite once
- `pnpm build`: compile each package to `dist`
- `pnpm typecheck`: strict effect-tsrs over every workspace and the root scripts
- `pnpm typecheck:tsc`: patched `tsc`. Run it when a checker pin changes.
- `pnpm typecheck:fast`: tsrs in seconds, without Effect diagnostics. `pnpm check` stays the gate.
- `pnpm lint`: type-aware lint and format check only
- `pnpm fix`: apply Oxfmt and safe Oxlint fixes. The fix deletes comment prose, so move what a comment knows into a name or a test first.
- `pnpm debt`: list every lint, Effect, and TypeScript directive with its reason. It exits non-zero while any directive exists. The list only shrinks.
- `pnpm verify`: run the long-running verification scripts of every package
- `pnpm verify:status`: print each Verify job's real outcome on the last ten commits on `main` (success, failure, cancelled, running, queued, or never ran, which means a newer push replaced it in the queue) and the newest finished result of every job. It exits non-zero while the newest finished result of any job is a failure. Run it after you push instead of reading `gh run list`, where a replaced run and a failed one look alike.
- `pnpm smoke <base-url>`: play one hand against a running site, local or deployed. It asks again every 5 seconds, for up to 3 minutes, while opening the first room answers `RoomUnavailable`. CI runs it against the production Worker on local workerd.
- `pnpm plan:<stage>`: show what a deploy of `production` or `preview` would change. Add `--first` before the first deploy. It never writes to Cloudflare.
- `pnpm deploy:<stage> --confirm=<stage>`: check, test, build, deploy, and smoke the stage. The owner approves every `production` deploy, each time. The lead agent may deploy `preview` from `main` when there is something worth seeing there. Delegated workers never deploy either stage: they report the command and what to check afterwards.
- `pnpm turbo run check test build`: the full gate. CI runs it after a frozen install.

### Verification scripts

A long-running check, such as an exhaustive enumeration or a simulator, is a script, not a test.

- Put it in `packages/<name>/scripts/<script>.ts`. The package tsconfig already includes `scripts/`, so the fence checks it.
- Add `"verify": "node scripts/<script>.ts"` to the package `scripts`. Node strips the types. Chain several scripts with `&&`.
- Import the package by its own name, for example `@nostakes/engine`. The name resolves to the built `dist`, and turbo's `verify` task builds the package first.
- Print with Effect `Console`, run the program with `Effect.runPromise`, and fail the Effect on a counterexample. A failed Effect exits non-zero.
- `verify` is not part of `check` or `test`, and turbo never caches it. Run `pnpm verify` before you commit a change to the rules a script covers.
- `apps/room-server` is the exception. Its probes need a deployed Worker, and only the `@alchemy.run/test` harness deploys one to local workerd, so its `verify` runs `scripts/*.verify.ts` through `vitest.verify.config.ts`. Each probe fails its Effect with a tagged error on a counterexample. `scripts/soak.verify.ts` plays at least 1,000 hands across at least 10 rooms, seven in ten of them checked, and its summary counts the bots' fair-shuffle verdicts by kind and the wait beyond the pause before each sealed deal. Set `SOAK_SEED`, `SOAK_HANDS`, and `SOAK_ROOMS` to replay or resize it. Rooms play in waves of `SOAK_CONCURRENCY` (4 by default), dealt round-robin so each wave mixes small and large tables, and each wave is judged after it stops playing and before the next one starts. Judging a room is synchronous and would freeze rooms still playing, and keeping every room's frames until the end makes memory grow with the number of rooms, so neither may come back. Each room prints its outcome as soon as its wave is judged, and the summary prints the peak heap and the event-loop stalls while rooms played. The soak stops itself after `SOAK_BUDGET_MINUTES` (95 by default, below the test's own limit) and still prints its summary, so a run that is too slow says what it saw instead of being killed silently. On every push to `main`, Verify plays a shorter soak with the other room-server probes: the same 12 room shapes with 160 hands, 6 at a time, within 20 minutes. The full 1,000 hands run as the `room-server full soak` job, 3 rooms at a time, every night and on manual dispatch. A failed room prints its seed and code and saves its stored log to a temporary directory, with a timeline beside it: the night's own steps and, for each client bot, its connection phases, its lines, every frame it sent, swallowed, or got, and each command with its outcome. Set `SOAK_KEEP_LOGS=true` to save every room's log and timeline. `scripts/hibernation.verify.ts` pings through a 30-second idle and checks the room still hibernates. `scripts/silence.verify.ts` waits about 60 seconds for a quiet socket to be closed as `Unresponsive` while a pinging one stays.
- `apps/web` runs `scripts/two-players.ts` and then `scripts/phones.ts` in Chromium as its `verify`. Both start the servers through `scripts/local-site.ts`. It starts the room server through `alchemy dev` and the web app through Vite on port 5199, then two headless Chromium windows launched with `--mute-audio` open a room, join, sit, start, peek and react while the other window watches the face look down and the reaction rise, and play a hand until a pot is awarded. Set `SIGNAL_SHOTS_DIR` to save a screenshot of each. In hand 2 it restarts the room server with a second `WEB_BUILD` and serves the pages that build from then on: both players must be told the new version loads after the hand, reload once, come back to their seats with every chip, and see hand 3 dealt. CI installs the headless shell first with `playwright-core install --with-deps --only-shell chromium`. Every automated browser in this repo is headless and muted. `scripts/phones.ts` opens two phone-sized touch windows and checks what a phone needs: the manifest, `theme-color` for both themes and for a chosen theme, no overscroll, selection, or double-tap zoom in the room, the safe-area insets through a devtools notch, the screen wake lock (held only while seated in a running game and visible, through a fake lock that counts requests and releases), the ping the client sends at once when the page is shown again, that the home screen drops a planted room the server does not have, and that a table defined after its first snapshot still draws it. Run `pnpm --filter @nostakes/web phones` to check WebKit as well; WebKit cannot emulate a notch or report a long-press callout. `SHOTS_DIR=<dir> pnpm --filter @nostakes/web shots` opens real rooms with 2, 6, and 9 players and photographs the home page, the invitation, and each table at 360 by 640, 390 by 844, and 844 by 390 in both themes, plus several boards from running it more than once through `scripts/layout/stage.html`, which feeds the real table element a snapshot. Look at them after a change to the layout.
- Integration tests that start workerd cost seconds on every commit through the hook. Keep `pnpm test` for the room server under about 30 seconds: one `beforeAll(deploy(ToyStack))` per file, files in up to four parallel workers (one fewer than the machine's cores), rooms that play at once rather than one long room, and slow probes such as the hibernation idle and the 60-second silence limit in `verify`.

## Fence

The fence makes cheating uncomfortable. If a hook fails, fix the failure. Do not disable the fence.

- Lefthook pre-commit runs `pnpm check` and `pnpm test`.
- Never skip the hooks: no `--no-verify`, no `commit -n`, no `LEFTHOOK=0`, no `core.hooksPath` override. The Claude Code hook in `.claude/settings.json` denies these commands. The policy lives in `scripts/vcs-command-policy.js`.
- `tsconfig.base.json` owns the Effect diagnostic severities. A package tsconfig may add an exact `allowedUnstableApis` entry only for a module the checker warns about. `engine` and `ledger` already allow `@effect/vitest` and `effect/Arbitrary`, because every test needs them. `apps/room-server` also allows the `effect/http` modules it uses, and `effect/FileSystem` and `@effect/platform-node/NodeFileSystem` for the soak's saved logs.
- Some rules collide with common Effect forms. Use the form that passes both:
  - `unicorn/no-array-method-this-argument` reads any two-argument `.map`, `.filter`, or `.forEach` as an array call with `thisArg`. `Option.map(option, f)` and `Effect.forEach(items, f)` trip it. Write `option.pipe(Option.map(f))` and `pipe(items, Effect.forEach(f))`. `Effect.forEach(f, { concurrency })` trips it too, so write `Effect.all(items.map(f), { concurrency })`.
  - `anti-slop-effect/no-service-constructor-imports` rejects importing any `make<Name>` outside a test file. Name a factory for what it returns, such as `hostRoom` or `roomObject`.
  - `no-empty-object-type` rejects an empty `Data.TaggedEnum` variant. Give every variant a field that says something.
  - `anti-slop-effect/no-manual-tagged-construction` rejects a `_tag` in an object literal, test fixtures included. Compare wire frames as JSON text when a test needs the literal form.
  - The Effect diagnostic `missingReturnYieldStar` wants `return yield*` on an Effect that never succeeds, and `consistent-return` rejects a generator that mixes `return;` with `return yield* ...`. Collect the outcome in a variable, leave the loop, and end with one `return yield* Option.match(outcome, { onNone: () => Effect.void, onSome: (problem) => Effect.fail(...) })`.
  - `promise/prefer-await-to-then` reads `Effect.catch` as a promise `.catch`, and `promise/prefer-await-to-callbacks` reads a callback whose parameter is named `error` as a Node callback. Use `Effect.mapError` or `Effect.catchTag`, and name the parameter `failure`.
  - `anti-slop/no-shape-in-symbol-names` rejects any name containing "shape". Name the thing for what it holds, such as `recipe` or `plan`.
  - A room view or rejection holds `Option` values, which plain `JSON.stringify` cannot carry. Wrap a core's schemas in `Schema.toCodecJson` before `Schema.fromJsonString`. `HostedRoom` and `ClientSchemas` take a `WireCodec`, whose encoded side is `Schema.Json`, so a schema that skips this does not typecheck.
  - `typescript/no-unsafe-type-assertion` rejects a narrowing assertion even with a `SAFETY:` comment. Narrow with a type predicate instead, as `src/workerd.ts` does for the globals only workerd defines.
  - A type-level test uses `expectTypeOf` from `@effect/vitest`, not `@ts-expect-error`, because every directive counts as debt.
- `no-redeclare` rejects Foldkit's habit of naming a schema and its type alike (`const Model` beside `type Model`). Name the schema `ModelSchema` and the Message union `Messages`, and keep `Model` and `Message` for the types.
- `xstate-effect/no-inline-effect` rejects an Effect returned from an inline `enq` callback and inline Effect logic passed to `enq.spawn`. Declare Effect work in `setupEffect({ actors })`.
- CI runs `pnpm install --frozen-lockfile` and then `pnpm turbo run check test build`.

## Project law

- Chips are integers. No floating point anywhere near a chip amount. Model amounts with an integer Schema. Never divide chips with `/`, and never repair a result with `Math.round`. Every split names its rule for the remainder.
- Pure domain packages (`engine`, `ledger`) do no I/O and read no clock or randomness. Inputs such as the shuffled deck arrive as data. The Effect diagnostics `globalDate`, `globalRandom`, `globalTimers`, `globalConsole`, `globalFetch`, `cryptoRandomUUID`, and `nodeBuiltinImport` are errors, and `nostakes-patterns/no-module-level-mutable-state` bans module-level `let` and `var`.
- Errors are part of the interface. Define each one as a `Schema.TaggedError`, for example `class SeatTaken extends Schema.TaggedError<SeatTaken>()("SeatTaken", { seat: Schema.Int }) {}`. Name the failure, keep its context, and say how to fix it.
- Schemas are the contract. Parse unknown input with Effect `Schema` at the boundary. Trust internal types after that. Do not validate the same value twice.
- No comments in code. `no-comments/no-comments` bans them. Agents copy comments, and a comment that explains a workaround spreads the workaround. Say it in a name, a type, a test, or the commit message. Three kinds survive:
  - A tool directive with its reason after `--`: `// oxlint-disable-next-line <rule> -- <reason>`, `// @effect-diagnostics-next-line <rule>:off -- <reason>`, or `// @ts-expect-error -- <reason>`
  - A one-line `SAFETY:` comment above a type assertion that names the invariant it relies on
  - A JSDoc block made only of type tags in a plain JS file
- Credit for borrowed code lives in `PROVENANCE.md`.
- Tests prove behaviour. Write Effect tests with `it.effect` and `it.layer` from `@effect/vitest`. `Effect.run*` and `ManagedRuntime.make` in a test file fail lint.
- Prefer properties and models. A domain rule is an `it.prop` or `it.effect.prop` over Schema or `effect/Arbitrary` inputs. Anything with history, such as a hand or a session, is a model checked over generated command sequences. Keep example tests for seams.
- Plant a bug before you trust a new test. The test must fail, then pass once you restore the code. Tautological tests and change-detector tests are harmful.
- Delete `passWithNoTests` from a package's `vitest.config.ts` when its first test lands.
- Lint baselines only shrink. Add a lint rule before you clean up a bad pattern.

## Architecture

- Dependencies run from `apps` to `packages`. `nostakes-boundaries/no-cross-layer-imports` stops a package from importing app code.
- `engine` owns the rules of a hand. `ledger` owns the money across a session. Neither imports the other yet.
- Pure domain state machines are plain reducers over tagged unions. `decide(state, command)` returns the events the command causes, or fails with a tagged error. `evolve(state, event)` returns the next state. Branch with `Match.tagsExhaustive` or a `Data.taggedEnum` `$match`. A `switch` on `_tag` fails lint (`anti-slop-effect/no-manual-tag-comparison`).
- `protocol` owns the wire, including the close reasons and their codes, the ping and pong texts, the ping interval, and the server's silence limit. `room-server` owns identity, storage, delivery, and limits, and depends on `protocol`. The pure room core plugs into the server through `HostedRoom` in one module and never sees a socket, a token, or a sequence number.
- Lifecycles with side effects are XState machines. This is a deliberate reading of rat-stack's "lifecycles are machines" rule. A pure reducer has no side effect to retry, cancel, or resume, so it needs no actor. A lifecycle that waits, times out, or talks to the network gets a machine.
- The room server has no XState machine. Its only lifecycle is "wait until a deadline, then act", and that deadline is data in the core's state (`nextDeadline`), re-read on every start and after every commit and armed as the Durable Object alarm. There is no hidden state for a machine to own. Every change to a room goes through one synchronous commit function in `src/room-host.ts` that cannot await.
- The client's connection lifecycle has hidden state and side effects, so it is one XState machine, `roomClientMachine` in `packages/client/src/machine.ts`. XState owns the states (Idle, Connecting, Greeting, Live, Waiting, Refused, Outdated, Stopped) and which transitions are legal. Declared Effect actors own the work. The Online state invokes the connection actor, so the socket lives exactly as long as Online. Time, backoff jitter, and the socket are the Effect services `Clock`, `Random`, and `Transport`, each with a live and a test layer. The embedding app sends `wake` when the page becomes visible or the network returns.
- Arm a machine timer as a named delayed event with an id, and cancel it on exit. XState's built-in `xstate.after` and `xstate.done.actor` events are not in a machine's event type, so a pure model test could only fire them through an unsafe cast. For the same reason a child actor relays named events to its parent instead of finishing with output.
- `Effect.sleep` inside a loop fails lint (`nostakes-patterns/no-sleep-in-loop`). Waiting belongs in a machine.

## Source control

- Trunk-based development. Commit small atomic changes straight to `main`. Every commit passes `pnpm check` and `pnpm test` through the hook.
- Use a conventional prefix (`feat:`, `fix:`, `test:`, `build:`, `ci:`, `docs:`, `chore:`) and an imperative subject under 72 characters. Add a body when the why is not obvious.
- Stage only the files your change touched. Never commit `dist`, `.turbo`, or secrets.

## Boundaries and sign-off

- Safe by default: code and tests inside a package, a new or tighter test or lint rule, a targeted override with a written reason, an `allowedUnstableApis` entry the checker asks for, a new verification script, and edits to `AGENTS.md` and `README.md`.
- Routine dependency bumps need no sign-off: a patch or minor release of something already pinned, with no breaking change for this repo, landed through the normal gate with CI and the Verify jobs green, and reported to the owner afterwards. Read the release notes first and keep every pin exact.
- Needs owner sign-off: adding or removing a dependency, a major version, a bump with a breaking change or a migration, and any new patch under `patches/`, because pins are exact and CI installs cold. Any edit that loosens `oxlint.config.ts`, the diagnostics in `tsconfig.base.json`, `lefthook.yml`, `.claude/settings.json`, or `scripts/vcs-command-policy.js`. A new package or app. Anything that brings I/O, a clock, or randomness into `engine` or `ledger`.
