How to host a poker night

Pick a buy-in, set the big blind at about a hundredth of it, agree on the house rules and an end time before the first hand, and write down every buy-in so the settle-up takes a minute.

The game here is a cash game of no-limit Texas hold'em: the blinds stay the same all night and players can come and go. Four to nine players is a good table. New to hold'em? Learn it in two minutes.

Blinds and buy-ins

What matters is how many big blinds each player starts with. Poker players call it stack depth. It decides what kind of game you get.

Start with What it plays like
40 big blinds Short. Lots of all-ins before and on the flop. Quick and loud, and forgiving for new players.
100 big blinds The usual cash game. Room to bet every street.
200 or more Deep. Big decisions after the flop, and mistakes cost more.

Pick the buy-in first, then the blinds. For 100 big blinds, divide the buy-in by 100 for the big blind and halve that for the small blind. A buy-in of 200 chips plays at blinds of 1 and 2. If your group settles up in money afterwards, pick a buy-in everyone at the table is happy to lose.

Rebuys

Agree on the smallest and largest buy-in, and on when a player may add chips: at any time up to the largest buy-in, only when below the smallest, or only when out of chips. A cap on the largest buy-in stops the deepest stack from bullying the table.

How long a night runs

A home game dealt by hand gets through roughly 20 to 30 hands an hour, fewer when people chat. An app deals faster. Three to four hours is a full night.

Set the end time when people arrive, not when someone is losing. When it comes, call the last orbit: play until the button has gone round the table once more, so everyone pays the blinds the same number of times. Agree too on whether a player who is up may leave early, and with how much warning.

House rules to agree on first

Each of these starts arguments when it is settled mid-hand.

  • The blinds, the smallest and largest buy-in, and when rebuys are allowed.
  • Whether an ante goes in, and who pays it.
  • Straddles: allowed or not, and how many.
  • Running it twice when players are all in.
  • Bomb pots: how big and how often.
  • The seven-deuce bounty, and its amount.
  • Showing cards: whether a winner must show at the showdown, whether a player may show one card, and whether you may see the cards that would have come.
  • How long a player may think before someone calls the clock.
  • How and when people settle up.

Keep the hand rankings handy for the newer players.

Settling up

Write down every buy-in and rebuy as it happens. At the end, each player's net is what they leave with minus everything they bought in for. The nets add up to zero. When they do not, a buy-in was missed.

Then make as few transfers as you can. A simple way: the biggest loser pays the biggest winner as much as one of them is owed, and you repeat until everyone is square.

Player Net
Ana +60
Ben +20
Cal −50
Dee −30

Cal pays Ana 50. Dee pays Ana 10 and Ben 20. Three transfers, and everyone is square. Settle the same night, while everyone remembers.

Hosting online

If your friends are not in one city, nostakes is one way to host. You open a room and send the link to the group chat. Up to nine people sit down, and every table rule in the list above is a setting the host picks. The room counts every buy-in, and at the end it shows each net and the fewest transfers that square everyone. It is free, nobody signs up, and it holds no money.
