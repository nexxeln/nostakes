How to host a poker night

Pick a buy-in, make the big blind a hundredth of it, agree on the rules and an end time, and write down every buy-in.

Blinds and buy-ins

What matters is how many big blinds everyone starts with.

Start with What it plays like
40 big blinds Short. Lots of all-ins. Quick, loud, and kind to new players.
100 big blinds The usual cash game.
200 or more Deep. Big decisions after the flop.

A buy-in of 200 plays at blinds of 1 and 2. If you settle in money, pick a buy-in everyone's happy to lose. Agree on rebuys too: any time up to the max, only below the min, or only when busted.

How long it runs

A hand-dealt game gets through 20 to 30 hands an hour, and 3 to 4 hours is a full night. Set the end time when people arrive, not when someone's losing. When it comes, play one last orbit.

Agree on these first

  • Blinds, buy-ins, and rebuys.
  • Antes, straddles, and bomb pots.
  • Running it twice and the seven-deuce bounty.
  • Who has to show at the showdown.
  • When to call the clock on a slow player.

Keep the hand rankings handy for newer players.

Settling up

Each net is what a player leaves with minus what they bought in. The nets add up to zero. If they don't, someone missed a buy-in. Then make as few transfers as you can.

Player Net
Ana +60
Ben +20
Cal −50
Dee −30

Cal pays Ana 50. Dee pays Ana 10 and Ben 20. Three transfers and everyone's square.

Hosting online

Friends in other cities? Open a room and send the link. Every rule above is a host setting, and at the end the room shows the fewest transfers that square everyone. Free, no sign-up, no money.
