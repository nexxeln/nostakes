How to play poker

Play five short hands with Mara, Bo and Jonas. Two minutes, and you can sit at a friend's table.

This is Texas hold'em, the game most friends play. Everything the five hands teach is written out here.

What happens in a hand

  1. Two players put in the blinds, a small one and a big one, so there is always something to win. That is the pot.
  2. Everyone gets two cards, face down. Only you see yours.
  3. There is a round of betting. On your turn you fold, call, or raise.
  4. Three cards are turned up for everyone to use: the flop. Then another round of betting.
  5. A fourth card is turned up: the turn. Betting again.
  6. A fifth card is turned up: the river. The last round of betting.
  7. Whoever is left shows their cards. You make your best five cards from your two and the five on the table, and the best five win the pot.

If everyone else folds before that, the last player left takes the pot and does not have to show.

What you can do on your turn

Check
Stay in without paying. You can only check when nobody has bet in this round. In nostakes, tap the chip.
Call
Match the bet in front of you. Tap the chip.
Bet or raise
Put chips in, or put in more than the last bet. Everyone after you has to match it or fold. Drag the chip up.
Fold
Give up your cards, and whatever you already put in. Flick your cards away.
All in
Bet everything in front of you. If a bet is more than you have, you can still call with what is left.

What beats what

Best first. You do not need to memorise it on day one: a pair is common, two pair is good, and anything from a straight up is strong.

  2. Q♠︎ Q♥︎ Q♦︎ Q♣︎ 4♠︎Four of a kindAll four of one rank.
  3. K♠︎ K♥︎ K♦︎ 6♣︎ 6♠︎Full houseThree of one rank and two of another.
  4. A♥︎ J♥︎ 8♥︎ 5♥︎ 2♥︎FlushFive of one suit.
  5. 9♠︎ 8♦︎ 7♥︎ 6♣︎ 5♥︎StraightFive in a row.
  6. 8♠︎ 8♥︎ 8♦︎ K♣︎ 3♠︎Three of a kindThree of one rank.
  7. J♠︎ J♦︎ 4♥︎ 4♣︎ A♠︎Two pairTwo different pairs.
  8. 10♠︎ 10♥︎ A♦︎ 7♣︎ 4♠︎One pairTwo of one rank.
  9. A♠︎ J♦︎ 8♥︎ 6♣︎ 3♠︎High cardNothing matches. The highest card plays.

When two players have the same kind of hand, the higher cards win: a pair of kings beats a pair of eights. With the same pair, the highest other card decides. It is called the kicker. Suits never break a tie, and if the best five cards are exactly the same, the pot is split.

Four habits that keep your chips

1. Fold most hands. Play pairs and big cards.

Most of the hands you are dealt are not worth playing, and folding them costs nothing unless you are in the blinds. Start with pairs and with two cards that are both ten or higher, and let the rest go.

2. Got a good hand? Bet it. Calling along bleeds chips.

When you have a good pair or better, bet. A bet wins the pot at once when the others fold, and it gets paid when they call. Calling bet after bet with a weak hand and hoping is how a stack disappears.

3. One card short? A cheap look is fine. An expensive one is not.

Four cards to a flush or a straight make it on the next card about one time in five. Call a small bet to see that card. Fold to a big one.

4. A big bet on the river usually means it.

By the last card nobody is hoping any more. A player who bets big there usually has the hand, and one pair is rarely enough to call.

What the hands left out

Where you sit

The dealer button moves one seat to the left after every hand, and the blinds move with it. Acting later in a round is an advantage, because you see what the others do first.

Bluffing

You can win without the best hand if everyone else folds. It works less often than films suggest, and hardly ever against someone who calls everything. Learn to bet your good hands first.

How much to bet

Between half the pot and the whole pot is normal. In no-limit you may bet any amount, up to everything in front of you.

Side pots

When someone is all in for less than the others have bet, the extra goes into a side pot that only the others can win. nostakes counts it for you.

Table manners

  • Act when it is your turn, not before.
  • Keep your cards to yourself while the hand is live, and do not say what you folded.
  • Say what you are doing: "call", "raise to 60".
  • Do not take long over small decisions.
  • Be kind to whoever is losing. Next week it is you.

Words you will hear

Blinds
The two forced bets that start every pot.
Button
The disc that marks the dealer. It moves one seat every hand.
Flop, turn, river
The three cards, then the fourth, then the fifth, turned up for everyone.
Pot
All the chips bet in this hand.
Kicker
Your highest card outside a pair. It breaks ties.
Showdown
Turning the cards over at the end to see who wins.
Muck
To throw your cards away without showing them.
Draw
A hand that needs one more card, such as four to a flush.
