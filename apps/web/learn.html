How to play poker

Play five short hands with Mara, Bo and Jonas. Two minutes, and you can sit at a friend's table.

This is Texas hold'em. Everything the five hands teach, written out.

What happens in a hand

  1. Two players post the blinds, a small one and a big one, so there's always a pot.
  2. Everyone gets two cards. Only you see yours.
  3. A round of betting. On your turn: fold, call, or raise.
  4. Three cards for everyone: the flop. Betting again.
  5. A fourth card: the turn. Betting again.
  6. A fifth card: the river. Last round of betting.
  7. Whoever's left shows. Best five cards from your two and the five on the table win.

If everyone else folds first, the last player takes the pot without showing.

What you can do on your turn

Check
Stay in for free, when nobody has bet. Tap the chip.
Call
Match the bet. Tap the chip.
Bet or raise
Put in chips, or more than the last bet. Drag the chip up.
Fold
Give up your cards and what you've put in. Flick your cards away.
All in
Bet everything. Short of a bet? You can still call with what's left.

What beats what

Best first. No need to memorise it today: a pair is common, two pair is good, a straight or better is strong.

Same kind of hand? Higher cards win: kings beat eights. Same pair? The best other card, the kicker, decides. Suits never break a tie. Exactly the same five cards split the pot.

Four habits that keep your chips

1. Fold most hands. Play pairs and big cards.

Most hands aren't worth playing, and folding is free unless you're in the blinds. Play pairs and two cards ten or higher.

2. Got a good hand? Bet it. Calling along bleeds chips.

A bet wins the pot when they fold and gets paid when they call. Calling bet after bet and hoping is how a stack disappears.

3. One card short? A cheap look is fine. An expensive one is not.

Four to a flush or a straight gets there about one time in five. Call a small bet to see the next card. Fold to a big one.

4. A big bet on the river usually means it.

By the last card nobody is hoping any more. One pair is rarely enough to call.

What the hands left out

Position
The button moves one seat left each hand. Acting last is an edge: you see what everyone else did.
Bluffing
You can win with nothing if everyone folds. It works less than films suggest, and never on someone who calls everything.
Bet size
Half the pot to the whole pot is normal. In no-limit you can bet anything up to your stack.
Side pots
When someone's all in for less, the extra goes in a side pot only the others can win. nostakes counts it for you.

Table manners

  • Act on your turn, not before.
  • Keep your cards to yourself, and don't say what you folded.
  • Don't take forever over small decisions.
  • Be kind to whoever's losing. Next week it's you.

Words you will hear

Blinds
The two forced bets that start every pot.
Button
The dealer's disc. It moves one seat every hand.
Kicker
Your best card outside a pair. It breaks ties.
Muck
Throw your cards away without showing them.
Draw
A hand one card short, like four to a flush.
