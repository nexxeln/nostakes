Poker hand rankings

Best to worst: royal flush, straight flush, four of a kind, full house, flush, straight, three of a kind, two pair, one pair, high card.

In Texas hold'em you make the best five-card hand you can from your two cards and the five on the table. You may use both of your cards, one, or none.

The ten hands, best first

The figure under each hand is how often your two cards and a full board make it, if you stay to the river.

  1. A♠︎ K♠︎ Q♠︎ J♠︎ 10♠︎

    Royal flush. Ace, king, queen, jack and ten, all one suit. It is the best straight flush.

    0.003% of the time

  2. 9♥︎ 8♥︎ 7♥︎ 6♥︎ 5♥︎

    Straight flush. Five in a row, all one suit.

    0.03%

  3. Q♠︎ Q♥︎ Q♦︎ Q♣︎ 4♠︎

    Four of a kind. All four cards of one rank. Also called quads.

    0.17%

  4. K♠︎ K♥︎ K♦︎ 6♣︎ 6♠︎

    Full house. Three of one rank and two of another. Also called a boat.

    2.6%

  5. A♥︎ J♥︎ 8♥︎ 5♥︎ 2♥︎

    Flush. Five of one suit, in any order.

    3.0%

  6. 9♠︎ 8♦︎ 7♥︎ 6♣︎ 5♥︎

    Straight. Five in a row, of any suits.

    4.6%

  7. 8♠︎ 8♥︎ 8♦︎ K♣︎ 3♠︎

    Three of a kind. Three of one rank. Also called trips, or a set when two of them are in your hand.

    4.8%

  8. J♠︎ J♦︎ 4♥︎ 4♣︎ A♠︎

    Two pair. Two cards of one rank and two of another.

    23.5%

  9. 10♠︎ 10♥︎ A♦︎ 7♣︎ 4♠︎

    One pair. Two cards of one rank.

    43.8%

  10. A♠︎ J♦︎ 8♥︎ 6♣︎ 3♠︎

    High card. Nothing matches. The highest card plays, then the next.

    17.4%

The order comes from five-card poker, where a rarer hand always ranks higher. With seven cards to choose from, one pair and two pair come up more often than no pair at all. The order stays the same.

Ties and kickers

When two players have the same kind of hand, compare the cards that make it, highest first. A pair of kings beats a pair of eights. If those match too, compare the other cards in the five, highest first. Those other cards are the kickers.

BoardK♥︎ 9♦︎ 7♣︎ 4♠︎ 2♥︎
YouA♦︎ K♣︎
ThemK♠︎ Q♠︎

You both have a pair of kings. Your ace beats their queen, so you win.

What to compare, hand by hand

Straight, straight flush
The top card of the straight. Nothing else counts.
Four of a kind
The rank of the four, then the fifth card.
Full house
The three first, then the pair. Kings full of sixes beats queens full of aces.
Flush
The highest card, then the next, down through all five.
Three of a kind
The three, then the two kickers in order.
Two pair
The higher pair, then the lower pair, then the fifth card.
One pair
The pair, then the three kickers in order.
High card
All five cards, highest first.

Only five cards play

A sixth card never breaks a tie. If your best five are the five on the board, everyone still in can play them too, and the best you can do is split.

BoardA♠︎ A♦︎ K♣︎ K♥︎ Q♠︎
YouJ♣︎ 10♦︎
Them9♠︎ 2♣︎

Both of you play aces and kings with a queen. Your jack is not in the best five, so the pot is split.

Exact ties split the pot

When the best five cards are the same rank for rank, the pot is split evenly. If it does not divide, groups give the odd chip in different ways. nostakes gives it to the winner closest to the left of the button.

The mix-ups that start arguments

A flush beats a straight

Five of one suit is rarer than five in a row, so it ranks higher. A full house beats both.

An ace can be low

The ace plays high or low in a straight. Ace to five is a straight, the lowest one, and counts as five high. Ten to ace is the highest.

5♣︎ 4♦︎ 3♥︎ 2♠︎ A♣︎

A straight cannot go round the corner. Queen, king, ace, two, three is not a straight.

Suits never break a tie

In hold'em no suit beats another. A flush in spades and a flush in hearts are compared by their ranks alone, and if those match, the pot is split.

Three pairs are still two pair

Seven cards can hold three pairs. You play the best two, and the best card left over is your kicker.

Four to a flush is nothing

A flush or a straight needs all five cards. Four of them is a draw, and at the showdown it counts as whatever else it makes.

Let the table read the hands

nostakes reads every hand at the showdown, names it, and pays the pot, side pots and split pots included. Nobody at the table has to argue about a kicker. New to the game? Learn hold'em by playing five short hands.
