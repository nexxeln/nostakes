The seven-deuce game

In the seven-deuce game, a player who wins a pot holding a seven and a two collects a bounty from the other players.

7♣︎ 2♦︎

Seven-deuce is the worst starting hand in hold'em. The bounty makes it worth playing, so people bluff with it, and nobody at the table can be sure what you hold.

How the bounty works

  1. Before the first hand, the group agrees on a bounty, counted in chips.
  2. You are dealt a seven and a two. The suits do not matter.
  3. You win the pot with them, at a showdown or because everyone else folded. You do not need the best hand. A bluff that gets folds counts.
  4. You show the seven-deuce, and the other players each pay you the bounty.

The bounty is paid on top of the pot. With six players and a bounty of 10, a seven-deuce win collects 50 more.

What changes at the table

Seven-deuce wins a showdown far less often than most hands, so its value is in bluffing. Holders raise it and keep betting. The others start calling down lighter, because a big bet might be seven-deuce. That makes bluffing with other hands harder, and value betting a little easier. Expect bigger pots and more stories.

Variations, and what to agree on first

Groups play the rule in different ways. Settle these before the first hand, not in the middle of an argument.

Who pays
Everyone dealt into the hand, everyone at the table, or only the players who put chips in the pot.
Showing
Almost every group requires the winner to show both cards to collect.
Split pots
Some pay the full bounty on a chop, some pay half, some pay nothing.
Side pots
Is winning the main pot enough when someone else takes a side pot?
Running it twice
Does winning one run count, or must seven-deuce win every run?
Bomb pots
Nobody chose to play a bomb pot hand, so many groups leave it out.
Short stacks
What a player who has less than the bounty pays.

How nostakes plays it

The bounty is a house rule the host turns on, under Showdown in the house rules or in the setup sheet before the first hand. The host sets the amount each player pays. It starts at 10 chips, five big blinds at the standard blinds of 1 and 2.

  • Seven-deuce is any seven with any two.
  • You are owed the bounty when you win, alone, every pot you were in, on every run of the board. Everyone folding to you counts. So does a showdown. So does a short stack that wins the main pot while others fight over a side pot it was not in.
  • A split pot pays nothing. Winning a side pot while losing the main pot pays nothing. When the board is run more than once, losing or splitting any run pays nothing.
  • Every other player dealt into the hand pays, folded players included. A player who holds less than the bounty pays all they have. A player with no chips left pays nothing. Players sitting out were not dealt in and do not pay.
  • You never have to claim it. If the table has not seen your cards, for example because everyone folded, the rule turns them face up. The bounty moves as the hand ends.
  • A bomb pot pays no bounty.
  • A player who is leaving the room, or whom the host removed, pays before they cash out.
  • The rules in force when the hand was dealt decide. Turning the bounty on or off during a hand counts from the next hand.

The bounty moves chips between stacks. It is not part of the pot, and every player's net for the night includes it. On the table, each payer's seat says Bounty −10 and the winner's says Bounty +10.
