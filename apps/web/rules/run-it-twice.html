Running it twice

When players are all in before the board is complete, running it twice means dealing the rest of the board two separate times. Each run wins half of the pot.

It does not change who is the favourite or what a hand is worth on average. It makes the result less all or nothing.

An example

Two players are all in after the flop, with 200 in the pot.

FlopK♦︎ 8♣︎ 3♠︎
AnaK♥︎ Q♥︎
Ben8♥︎ 8♦︎

Ben has three eights and Ana needs help. They agree to run it twice. The dealer deals a turn and a river, then deals another turn and river from the cards left in the deck. Each run decides who wins 100. If Ben wins one run and Ana the other, they each take 100 back.

What it does to the odds

Your chance of winning each run is the same as your chance of winning a single run, because the cards for every run come from the same unseen deck. So the expected value, what the hand is worth on average, does not change.

What changes is the spread. Say you are 80% to win a 200 pot. Run it once: 80% of the time you win 200, 20% of the time you get nothing. Run it twice: you win both runs about 64% of the time, split about 32% of the time, and lose both about 4% of the time. Either way the hand is worth 160 on average. Running it twice makes losing everything rarer, and winning everything rarer too.

That is why players who are behind like it and players who are ahead sometimes refuse. Neither gains or loses on average. The favourite gives up some chance of winning it all to cut the chance of losing it all.

When it is offered

  • Only when the betting is over because players are all in, and only before the river. Once all five cards are out there is nothing left to run.
  • The players in the hand agree on it. In most games one player saying no means one run.
  • Some groups run it three times or more. The pot is split into that many parts.
  • Groups differ on where odd chips go when the pot does not divide by the number of runs. Agree on it once.

How nostakes does it

Running it more than once is a house rule the host sets, under All in in the house rules or in the setup sheet before the first hand. It is off by default.

Run it up to
The most runs allowed, up to 9. 1 turns it off.
Runs
Vote: every player still in picks how many runs, up to the most, and the fewest picked wins. A player who lets the vote clock run out picks once. Always most: no vote, the board always runs the most times allowed.
  • The vote comes when two or more players are still in, at most one of them can still bet, and the board is not complete. Any chips nobody could call go back first.
  • Every run gets its own cards from the deck, so no card shows up on two runs. The board cards already out are shared by every run. When the deck is too short for the runs asked, the room runs as many as it can.
  • Each pot, the main pot and every side pot, is split into equal parts, one for each run. When a pot does not divide evenly, the first runs take the odd chips, one each. Within a run, a tie splits as usual.
  • By default every hand still in is turned face up before the board is run. The host can keep them hidden until the board is complete.
  • In a bomb pot every run shares the flop. A seven-deuce bounty is paid only when seven-deuce wins every run.
